# Profit and Loss

> Read this like a class, not like a formula sheet. Every idea comes with a picture in your head, then a solved example, then one for you to try on paper. Do the "Your turn" problems before reading the answer.

## 0. Start here: what is this topic really about?

Imagine a trader who buys a shirt for Rs 400 and sells it for Rs 500. He made Rs 100 on Rs 400. That is 25% profit. Flip it: buys for Rs 500 and sells for Rs 400. He lost Rs 100 on Rs 500. That is 20% loss.

This whole topic is about the gap between what you paid and what you received, expressed as a percentage of what you **paid** (not what you received).

- **What the exam gives you:** cost price (CP), selling price (SP), or one of them plus a percent.
- **What it asks for:** the other one of CP or SP, the profit/loss percent, or the marked price after a discount.
- **Typical exam twists:** false weights, buying at one rate and selling at another, two articles sold at same SP one at profit and one at loss.

**How many marks to expect:** SSC CGL 2 to 4 questions. RRB NTPC/Group D 2 to 3. IBPS/SBI PO 3 to 5. APPSC 2 to 4. This is one of the highest-scoring topics if you drill the types.

## 1. The only ideas you must understand

### The three central terms

- **CP (Cost Price):** what the trader paid to buy or make the article.
- **SP (Selling Price):** what the customer paid to buy it.
- **Profit or Loss:** the difference between SP and CP, and always expressed as a percentage of **CP**.

$$\text{Profit} = \text{SP} - \text{CP} \quad (\text{when SP > CP})$$

$$\text{Loss} = \text{CP} - \text{SP} \quad (\text{when CP > SP})$$

$$\text{Profit\%} = \dfrac{\text{Profit}}{\text{CP}} \times 100 \quad \quad \text{Loss\%} = \dfrac{\text{Loss}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$$

### Going from CP to SP

$$\text{SP} = \text{CP}\times\dfrac{100+\text{Profit\%}}{100} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{SP} = \text{CP}\times\dfrac{100-\text{Loss\%}}{100}$$

### Going from SP to CP

$$\text{CP} = \text{SP}\times\dfrac{100}{100+\text{Profit\%}} \quad \text{or} \quad \text{CP} = \text{SP}\times\dfrac{100}{100-\text{Loss\%}}$$

**Example:** CP Rs 450, 20% profit. SP $= 450\times\dfrac{120}{100} = \mathbf{Rs\ 540}$.

**Example:** SP Rs 480, 20% profit. CP $= 480\times\dfrac{100}{120} = \mathbf{Rs\ 400}$.

### Marked Price and Discount

Shopkeepers often write a **Marked Price** (MP) on the tag - higher than CP - then offer a discount.

$$\text{SP} = \text{MP}\times\dfrac{100-\text{Discount\%}}{100}$$

**Sense check:** profit% and loss% are always on CP. Discount% is always on MP. Mixing these up is the most common error in this chapter.

## 2. The shortcuts / tricks

### Shortcut 1: the multiplier method

Instead of computing profit in rupees and then finding percent, just compare the two multipliers:

CP $\times$ (profit multiplier) = SP. Multiplier $= \dfrac{100+p}{100}$ or $\dfrac{100-p}{100}$.

For a chain (mark up then discount): SP = CP $\times \dfrac{100+m}{100} \times \dfrac{100-d}{100}$.

### Shortcut 2: same SP, one profit one loss (critical exam trick)

Whenever two articles are sold at the **same SP**, one at $P\%$ profit and the other at $P\%$ loss, the transaction always results in a **net loss**:

$$\text{Net Loss\%} = \dfrac{P^2}{100}$$

**Why it works:** the CP for the profit article is lower (so you gained less) and the CP for the loss article is higher (so you lost more). The losses always outweigh the gains.

**Example:** two articles each sold at Rs 2400, one at 20% profit and one at 20% loss.

CP of profit article $= 2400/1.2 = 2000$. CP of loss article $= 2400/0.8 = 3000$.

Total CP $= 5000$. Total SP $= 4800$. Net loss $= 200$. Loss\% $= 200/5000\times100 = \mathbf{4\%}$.

Formula check: $20^2/100 = 4\%$. Correct.

### Shortcut 3: false weight profit

A trader **claims** to sell at cost price but uses a **false weight** (gives less than stated). The profit formula is:

$$\text{Profit\%} = \dfrac{\text{True weight} - \text{False weight}}{\text{False weight}} \times 100$$

**Example:** a shopkeeper uses 800g when the scale should read 1000g, but charges for 1000g.

$\dfrac{1000-800}{800}\times100 = \dfrac{200}{800}\times100 = \mathbf{25\%}$.

## 3. Types of questions and how to crack each one

### Type 1. Find profit% or loss%

**How you spot it:** both CP and SP are given. "A man buys for Rs 500 and sells for Rs 650. Find profit%."

**Think:** find the difference, then divide by CP and multiply by 100.

**Example 1.** CP Rs 500, SP Rs 650. Profit $= 150$. Profit\% $= \dfrac{150}{500}\times100 = \mathbf{30\%}$.

**Example 2.** CP Rs 800, SP Rs 680. Loss $= 120$. Loss\% $= \dfrac{120}{800}\times100 = \mathbf{15\%}$.

**Your turn:** CP Rs 480, SP Rs 600. Profit%? (Answer: $\dfrac{120}{480}\times100=25\%$)

### Type 2. Find SP given CP and percent

**How you spot it:** CP is given, asked for SP at a given profit% or loss%.

**Think:** multiply CP by $\dfrac{100+p}{100}$ (profit) or $\dfrac{100-p}{100}$ (loss).

**Example 1.** CP Rs 320, sell at 25% profit. SP $= 320\times\dfrac{125}{100} = \mathbf{Rs\ 400}$.

**Example 2.** CP Rs 480, sell at 12.5% loss. SP $= 480\times\dfrac{87.5}{100} = 480\times\dfrac{7}{8} = \mathbf{Rs\ 420}$.

**Your turn:** CP Rs 360, sell at 25% profit. SP? (Answer: $360\times1.25=Rs\ 450$)

### Type 3. Find CP given SP and percent

**How you spot it:** SP is given, asked for CP. "Sold at Rs 660 at a 10% profit. What was the CP?"

**Think:** divide SP by the multiplier $\dfrac{100+p}{100}$.

**Example 1.** SP Rs 660, 10% profit. CP $= 660\div1.1 = \mathbf{Rs\ 600}$.

**Example 2.** SP Rs 390, 25% loss. CP $= 390\div0.75 = \mathbf{Rs\ 520}$.

**Exam twist:** "a person sells at 10% loss. If he sold at Rs 50 more, he would make 15% profit. Find CP."

Let CP $= x$. $0.9x + 50 = 1.15x \implies 50 = 0.25x \implies x = \mathbf{Rs\ 200}$.

**Your turn:** SP Rs 700, 12.5% loss. CP? (Answer: $700\div0.875=Rs\ 800$)

### Type 4. Mark up and discount

**How you spot it:** "a shopkeeper marks 25% above CP and sells at 10% discount. Find profit%."

**Think:** $SP = MP \times (1-d/100) = CP \times (1+m/100) \times (1-d/100)$.

**Example.** CP Rs 750, mark up 20% (=Rs 900), give 20% discount.

SP $= 900\times0.8 = 720$. Loss $= 750-720 = 30$. Loss\% $= \dfrac{30}{750}\times100 = \mathbf{4\%}$.

**Another.** CP Rs 640, mark 25% above (=Rs 800), give 5% discount.

SP $= 800\times0.95 = 760$. Profit $= 120$. Profit\% $= \dfrac{120}{640}\times100 = \mathbf{18.75\%}$.

**Exam twist:** given SP and discount%, find MP. MP $= \text{SP}\div(1-d/100)$. If SP $= 756$ after 10% disc, MP $= 756\div0.9 = Rs\ 840$.

**Your turn:** CP Rs 400, marked 25% up, sold at 20% discount. Profit or loss%? (Answer: $SP=500\times0.8=400=CP$, so **0% profit**. Net change formula: $25-20-5=0\%$.)

### Type 5. Successive discounts

**How you spot it:** "two successive discounts of 20% and 10% on an item marked Rs 500."

**Think:** apply discounts one after the other. $\text{SP} = \text{MP}\times\dfrac{80}{100}\times\dfrac{90}{100}$.

**Example.** MP Rs 500, discounts 20% and 10%.

SP $= 500\times0.8\times0.9 = \mathbf{Rs\ 360}$. Equivalent single discount $= (1-0.8\times0.9)\times100 = 28\%$.

**Your turn:** discounts 15% and 20% on MP Rs 600. Final SP? (Answer: $600\times0.85\times0.80=Rs\ 408$)

### Type 6. False weight questions

**How you spot it:** "a trader claims to sell at cost but uses a 900g weight instead of 1kg."

**Think:** the customer pays for 1000g but receives only 900g. The trader's CP is for 900g.

$$\text{Profit\%} = \dfrac{\text{True weight} - \text{False weight}}{\text{False weight}}\times100$$

**Example.** Trader gives 900g for every 1000g at cost price.

Profit\% $= \dfrac{100}{900}\times100 = 11.11\%$.

**Example.** Trader gives 750g for every 1000g at cost price.

Profit\% $= \dfrac{250}{750}\times100 = 33.33\%$.

**Your turn:** shopkeeper gives 800g instead of 1000g at cost price. Profit%? (Answer: $\dfrac{200}{800}\times100=25\%$)

### Type 7. Buy at one rate, sell at another

**How you spot it:** "buys 12 oranges for Rs 10, sells 10 for Rs 15."

**Think:** find CP per unit and SP per unit, then compute profit%.

**Example.** Buys 12 oranges for Rs 10 (CP $= 10/12$). Sells 10 for Rs 12 (SP $= 12/10$).

Profit\% $= \dfrac{12/10 - 10/12}{10/12}\times100 = \dfrac{1.2-0.8\overline{3}}{0.8\overline{3}}\times100 = \mathbf{44\%}$.

**Example.** Buys 15 oranges for Rs 10 (CP $= 2/3$). Sells 10 for Rs 15 (SP $= 3/2$).

Profit\% $= \dfrac{3/2-2/3}{2/3}\times100 = \dfrac{9/6-4/6}{4/6}\times100 = \dfrac{5/6}{4/6}\times100 = \dfrac{5}{4}\times100 = \mathbf{125\%}$.

**Your turn:** buys 5 articles for Rs 4, sells 4 for Rs 5. Profit%? (Answer: CP $=4/5$, SP $=5/4$. Profit\% $=\dfrac{5/4-4/5}{4/5}\times100=\dfrac{9/20}{4/5}\times100=\dfrac{9}{16}\times100=56.25\%$)

### Type 8. Same SP, profit and loss at same percentage

**How you spot it:** "two articles, each sold at Rs 1200. One at 20% profit, other at 20% loss."

**Think:** use the formula. Net loss\% $= P^2/100$.

**Example.** Each sold at Rs 2400, at 20% profit and 20% loss.

Net loss\% $= 20^2/100 = \mathbf{4\%}$.

Total CP $= 2400/1.2 + 2400/0.8 = 2000+3000 = 5000$. Net loss $= 5000-4800 = 200$. Loss\% $= 4\%$.

**Your turn:** each sold at Rs 960, one at 25% profit, other at 25% loss. Net loss%? (Answer: $25^2/100=6.25\%$)

### Type 9. Fraction of articles sold at different profits

**How you spot it:** "2/3 of the goods sold at 30% profit, the rest at 10% loss. Overall profit%?"

**Think:** take total CP as a round number (say 300). Calculate SP of each part, then find overall.

**Example.** CP $= 300$. Sell $\dfrac{2}{3}$ (i.e., 200 worth) at 30% profit: SP $= 260$. Sell remaining 100 at 10% loss: SP $= 90$. Total SP $= 350$.

Overall profit\% $= \dfrac{350-300}{300}\times100 = \mathbf{16.\overline{6}\%}$.

**Your turn:** 2/5 sold at 20% loss, 3/5 at 30% profit. Overall? (Answer: CP $=100$. $40\times0.8 + 60\times1.3 = 32+78=110$. Profit $=10\%$)

### Type 10. CP with overhead

**How you spot it:** "bought for Rs 540, spent Rs 60 on repair, sold at 25% profit."

**Think:** the effective CP is the purchase price plus any overheads. Apply the profit% on **total** CP.

**Example.** CP Rs 540, overheads Rs 60. Total CP $= 600$. Sell at 25% profit.

SP $= 600\times1.25 = \mathbf{Rs\ 750}$.

**Your turn:** buys for Rs 1200, spends Rs 150 on transport. Sells at 20% profit. SP? (Answer: total CP $=1350$. SP $=1350\times1.2=Rs\ 1620$)

### Type 11. Loss and profit - change in SP

**How you spot it:** "selling at Rs X makes a 10% loss. At what SP would there be a 20% profit?"

**Think:** from SP and loss%, find CP first. Then compute the new SP.

**Example.** SP Rs 180 makes 10% loss. CP $= 180\div0.9 = 200$. SP for 15% profit $= 200\times1.15 = \mathbf{Rs\ 230}$.

**Your turn:** SP Rs 540 makes a loss of Rs 60. At what SP will he make 20% profit? (Answer: CP $=540+60=600$. SP for 20\% profit $=600\times1.2=Rs\ 720$)

### Type 12. Gain on selling a number of articles

**How you spot it:** "on selling 30 articles, the gain equals the CP of 10 articles."

**Think:** gain $= 10\times\text{CP per article}$. Total CP $= 30\times\text{CP per article}$. Profit\% $= 10/30\times100$.

**Example.** Gain on selling 50 articles = SP of 10 articles.

Gain $= 50\times\text{SP} - 50\times\text{CP} = 10\times\text{SP}$.

$50\text{SP} - 50\text{CP} = 10\text{SP} \implies 40\text{SP} = 50\text{CP} \implies \text{SP/CP} = 5/4$.

Profit\% $= \dfrac{5/4-1}{1}\times100 = \mathbf{25\%}$.

**Example.** Gain on selling 30 articles = CP of 10 articles. Profit\% $= 10/30\times100 = 33.33\%$.

**Your turn:** by selling 40 articles, a trader gains equal to CP of 10 articles. Profit%? (Answer: profit $=10\times$CP$_\text{unit}$, total CP $=40\times$CP$_\text{unit}$, profit\% $=25\%$)

### Type 13. Two items, sum of CP given

**How you spot it:** "A and B together cost Rs 2000. A sold at 20% profit, B at 10% loss. Net profit Rs 100."

**Think:** set up two equations: $A+B = \text{total}$ and $0.2A - 0.1B = \text{net profit}$.

**Example.** $A+B = 2000$, $0.2A-0.1B = 100$.

$0.2A - 0.1(2000-A) = 100 \implies 0.3A = 300 \implies A = \mathbf{Rs\ 1000}$, $B = \mathbf{Rs\ 1000}$.

**Your turn:** two articles cost Rs 3000 total. First sold at 15% profit, second at 10% loss. Net gain Rs 150. Find CP of each. (Answer: $0.15A-0.10B=150$, $A+B=3000$. $0.15A-0.10(3000-A)=150$. $0.25A=450$. $A=1800$, $B=1200$.)

### Type 14. Loss equals fraction of SP

**How you spot it:** "loss is 1/5 of the selling price. Find loss%."

**Think:** express CP in terms of SP, then find loss%.

**Example.** Loss $= \dfrac{1}{5}$ of SP. CP = SP $+$ loss = SP $+ \dfrac{1}{5}$SP $= \dfrac{6}{5}$SP.

Loss\% $= \dfrac{\text{loss}}{\text{CP}}\times100 = \dfrac{SP/5}{6SP/5}\times100 = \dfrac{1}{6}\times100 = \mathbf{16.\overline{6}\%}$.

**Your turn:** profit $= \dfrac{1}{4}$ of SP. Find profit%? (Answer: CP =SP $-$ SP/4 $= 3$SP$/4$. Profit\% $= (SP/4)/(3SP/4)\times100 = 33.33\%$)

## 4. One page to revise before the exam

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Find profit\% / loss\% | $(SP-CP)/CP\times100$ or $(CP-SP)/CP\times100$ |
| Find SP from CP and \% | $CP\times(100\pm p)/100$ |
| Find CP from SP and \% | $SP\times100/(100\pm p)$ |
| Mark up then discount | $CP\times(100+m)/100\times(100-d)/100$ |
| Find MP from SP and discount | $SP\div(1-d/100)$ |
| Successive discounts $a\%$ and $b\%$ | Apply one after another; equiv disc $=a+b-ab/100$ |
| Same SP, one profit one loss at same \% | Net loss $=P^2/100$ |
| False weight | Profit\% $=(E-G)/G\times100$ where $G$ is given, $E$ is expected |
| Buy at one rate, sell at another | Find CP/unit and SP/unit, then \% |
| Gain = CP of $n$ articles (selling $m$) | Profit\% $=n/m\times100$ |
| Cost $+$ overhead | Add all costs to get effective CP |
| Loss = fraction of SP | Express CP in terms of SP, then compute |

### Key percent-fraction equivalents for this chapter

| Multiplier | Means |
|---|---|
| $\times 1.10$ | 10% profit |
| $\times 0.90$ | 10% loss |
| $\times 1.25$ | 25% profit |
| $\times 0.75$ | 25% loss |
| $\times 1.20$ | 20% profit |
| $\times 0.80$ | 20% loss |
| $\div 1.20$ | find CP when 20% profit is known |
| $\div 0.80$ | find CP when 20% loss is known |

## 5. Mistakes that cost marks

1. **Profit\% on SP instead of CP.** The denominator in profit\% is ALWAYS CP, not SP. Never divide by SP.
2. **Discount\% on CP instead of MP.** Discount is always on Marked Price.
3. **Same SP, different \% means same net change:** wrong. Same SP at same P\% profit and loss always gives a loss of $P^2/100\%$.
4. **Treating overhead as profit.** Add transport, repair, loading costs to CP before computing profit%.
5. **Adding successive discounts.** 20% and 10% discount is not 30%. It is $20+10-2=28\%$ equivalent. Apply them one by one.
6. **False weight profit on selling price.** Profit from false weight is on the weight given (false weight), not on the weight claimed. Use the formula $(E-G)/G\times100$.
7. **Reversing the "gain on selling $n$ articles" logic.** If gain = CP of $k$ articles out of $n$ sold, profit\% $= k/n\times100$.
