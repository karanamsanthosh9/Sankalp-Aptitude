# Ratio and Proportion

> Read this like a class, not like a formula list. Every idea comes with a picture in your head, then a solved example, then one for you to try. Do the "Your turn" boxes with a pen before reading the answer next to them.

## 0. Start here: what is this topic really about?

Imagine you are making tea for a crowd. The recipe says: 2 cups of milk for every 3 cups of water. Whether you make 5 cups or 500 cups, the **taste stays the same** as long as milk and water stay in that same relationship. That relationship is a **ratio**. Keeping two ratios equal is called **proportion**.

The questions in this chapter look like these:

- "Divide Rs 3900 among A, B and C in the ratio 2 : 3 : 8."
- "Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 8 is added to each, the ratio becomes 5 : 7. Find the numbers."
- "The ages of a father and son are in the ratio 7 : 2. After 12 years the ratio will be 9 : 4. Find their present ages."
- "Incomes are in the ratio 5 : 4, expenditures in the ratio 3 : 2, and each saves Rs 1600. Find the incomes."
- "Find the fourth proportional to 4, 6 and 16."

**What the exam gives you:** two or three quantities in a ratio, plus one extra clue (a total, a difference, a change, or a saving). **What it asks for:** the actual quantities, a new ratio, or a missing term.

**How many marks to expect:** SSC CGL and CHSL, RRB NTPC and Group D, IBPS and SBI PO and Clerk, APPSC and PSU papers carry about 2 to 4 questions directly from this chapter. It also hides inside Partnership, Mixtures, Ages, Profit and Loss and Time and Work, so mastering it pays many times over.

## 1. The only ideas you must understand

### What a ratio really says

A **ratio** compares two quantities **of the same kind** by division. If Ravi has Rs 40 and Sita has Rs 60, the ratio of their money is $40 : 60$. Divide both by 20 and you get $2 : 3$.

Picture it as blocks. Ravi has 2 blocks, Sita has 3 blocks, and every block is worth Rs 20. The numbers 2 and 3 are called **parts**. The real money is "parts times the value of one part".

$$\text{Actual quantity} = \text{Parts} \times \text{Value of one part}$$

This single line solves most of the chapter.

- A ratio has **no unit**. Rs 40 : Rs 60 is just $2 : 3$.
- Both terms must be in the **same unit** before you compare. 2 hours : 45 minutes is $120 : 45 = 8 : 3$, not $2 : 45$.
- Multiplying or dividing **both** terms by the same number does not change the ratio.
- A ratio is the same thing as a fraction: $a : b = \dfrac{a}{b}$.

### What a proportion says

Four numbers $a, b, c, d$ are **in proportion** when $a : b = c : d$, which we also write $a : b :: c : d$. In fraction form:

$$\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad a\times d = b\times c$$

The first and last terms ($a$ and $d$) are the **extremes**. The middle two ($b$ and $c$) are the **means**. The rule is: **product of extremes = product of means**. This is cross-multiplication, and you already know it.

### Trying it: divide Rs 500 in the ratio 2 : 3

Step 1. Add the parts: $2 + 3 = 5$ parts.

Step 2. One part $= 500 \div 5 = \text{Rs }100$.

Step 3. First share $= 2 \times 100 = \text{Rs }200$. Second share $= 3 \times 100 = \text{Rs }300$.

**Answer: Rs 200 and Rs 300.**

**Sense check you should always do:** the shares must add back to the total (200 + 300 = 500), and the bigger part must get the bigger share. If either fails, stop and redo.

### Your turn

Divide Rs 48 in the ratio 1 : 3. (Answer: Rs 12 and Rs 36. Four parts, one part is Rs 12.)

## 2. The shortcuts that save time in every question

### Shortcut 1. Think in "parts" and find the value of ONE part

Almost every ratio question gives you one real number (a total, a difference or one share). Divide it by the matching number of parts. That gives one part. After that, everything is multiplication.

Two numbers in the ratio $7 : 4$ differ by 24. The difference is $7 - 4 = 3$ parts, so 3 parts $=24$ and one part $=8$. The numbers are $56$ and $32$.

### Shortcut 2. The "k" method

When a question gives a ratio like $3 : 5$ and a condition, write the numbers as $3k$ and $5k$. Put them in the condition. Solve for $k$. Then multiply back.

This is the tool for almost every "if 8 is added to each" question. You will see it in Types 7, 8, 9 and 12.

### Shortcut 3. Making the common term equal (for $A:B$ and $B:C$)

If $A : B = 2 : 3$ and $B : C = 4 : 5$, the two B values (3 and 4) do not match. Take the **LCM** of 3 and 4, which is 12. Rewrite both ratios so B is 12.

- $A : B = 2 : 3 = 8 : 12$ (multiplied by 4)
- $B : C = 4 : 5 = 12 : 15$ (multiplied by 3)

So $A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15$. Check: $A : C$ from this is $8 : 15$, and $\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{4}{5}=\dfrac{8}{15}$. It matches.

### Shortcut 4. Comparing two ratios by cross-multiplying

To decide which of $\dfrac{5}{8}$ and $\dfrac{7}{11}$ is bigger, do not convert to decimals. Cross-multiply:

- $5 \times 11 = 55$ and $7 \times 8 = 56$.
- The side with the bigger product has the bigger fraction. $56 > 55$, and 56 came from the 7, so $\dfrac{7}{11}$ is bigger.

**Why it works:** you are putting both fractions over the same denominator $8\times11$ and comparing the tops.

### Shortcut 5. Direct and inverse in one line

- If one thing goes up and the other goes up in the **same ratio** (pens and cost), they are in **direct** proportion. Divide to get the rate.
- If one goes up and the other comes down (men and days), they are in **inverse** proportion. The **product** stays constant.

### Your turn

Which is larger, $\dfrac{9}{14}$ or $\dfrac{7}{11}$? (Answer: $\dfrac{9}{14}$. Cross products: $9\times11=99$ and $7\times14=98$.)

## 3. Types of questions and how to crack each one

Each type below has: how you spot it, how to think about it, a worked example, an exam twist where one exists, and a question for you.

### Type 1. Simplifying and comparing ratios

**How you spot it:** "Simplify 48 : 72", "Which ratio is greater?", "Arrange in ascending order".

**Think:** simplify by dividing out the HCF. For fractions, clear the denominators first. Convert units before you compare.

**Example 1 (simplify).** $48 : 72$.
- HCF of 48 and 72 is 24.
- $48\div24 = 2$ and $72\div24=3$.
- **Answer: $2 : 3$.**

**Example 2 (units).** Ratio of 2 hours to 45 minutes.
- 2 hours $=120$ minutes.
- $120 : 45$, HCF 15, gives **$8 : 3$**.

**Example 3 (fractions).** Simplify $\dfrac{2}{3} : \dfrac{5}{6}$.
- Multiply both by the LCM of denominators, which is 6: $4 : 5$.
- **Answer: $4 : 5$.**

**Example 4 (ordering).** Arrange $\dfrac{3}{4}$, $\dfrac{5}{6}$, $\dfrac{7}{9}$ from smallest to largest.
- LCM of 4, 6, 9 is 36. Multiply each by 36: $27$, $30$, $28$.
- Order: $27 < 28 < 30$, so $\dfrac{3}{4} < \dfrac{7}{9} < \dfrac{5}{6}$.

**Your turn:** Which is the largest of $\dfrac{2}{3}$, $\dfrac{3}{5}$, $\dfrac{5}{8}$? (Answer: $\dfrac{2}{3}$. Multiply by 120: $80$, $72$, $75$.)

### Type 2. Dividing a quantity in a given ratio

**How you spot it:** "Divide Rs 3900 among A, B, C in the ratio 2 : 3 : 8."

**Think:** add the parts, find one part, multiply.

**Example 1.** Rs 3900 in the ratio $2 : 3 : 8$.
- Parts: $2+3+8=13$. One part $=3900\div13=300$.
- Shares: $600$, $900$, $2400$.
- **Answer: Rs 600, Rs 900 and Rs 2400.**

**Example 2 (fraction form).** Divide Rs 1300 between A and B so that A gets $\dfrac{3}{2}$ times as much as B.
- "A gets $\dfrac{3}{2}$ times B's share" means $A : B = 3 : 2$. Parts $=5$, so one part $=1300\div5=260$.
- A $=3\times260$ = **Rs 780**, B $=2\times260$ = **Rs 520**.

**Exam twist:** "Find the difference between the largest and smallest share." In Example 1 that is $8-2=6$ parts $=6\times300=\text{Rs }1800$. No need to compute every share.

**Your turn:** Divide Rs 7700 in the ratio $5 : 6$. What is the larger share? (Answer: Rs 4200. Eleven parts, one part is Rs 700.)

### Type 3. Finding the numbers from a total, a difference, or one known share

**How you spot it:** "Two numbers are in the ratio 7 : 4 and differ by 24", "Boys : girls is 5 : 3 and there are 45 girls. How many students in all?"

**Think:** match the real number to the right number of parts. Difference goes with the difference of parts. A known share goes with its own part.

**Example 1 (difference).** Ratio $7 : 4$, difference 24. Find the larger.
- Difference in parts $=3$. One part $=24\div3=8$.
- Larger $=7\times8$ = **56**. (Smaller $=32$.)

**Example 2 (one share known).** Boys : girls $=5 : 3$ and there are 45 girls. Total students?
- 3 parts $=45$, so one part $=15$.
- Total $=8$ parts $=8\times15$ = **120**.

**Example 3 (total).** Two numbers in the ratio $3 : 5$ add up to 96.
- 8 parts $=96$, one part $=12$. Numbers: **36 and 60**.

**Your turn:** Two numbers are in the ratio $5 : 9$ and differ by 36. Find the larger. (Answer: 81. Four parts $=36$, one part $=9$.)

### Type 4. Combining ratios: $A:B$ and $B:C$, and compounded ratio

**How you spot it:** "$A:B=2:3$ and $B:C=4:5$. Find $A:B:C$." Also "the compounded ratio of 2 : 3 and 5 : 6".

**Think:** for a chain, make the common middle term equal using the LCM. For a **compounded ratio**, simply multiply the first terms together and multiply the second terms together.

**Example 1 (chain).** $A:B=2:3$ and $B:C=4:5$.
- LCM of 3 and 4 is 12.
- $A:B=8:12$ and $B:C=12:15$.
- **$A:B:C=8:12:15$.**

**Example 2 (compounded).** Compounded ratio of $2:3$ and $5:6$.
- $\dfrac{2\times5}{3\times6}=\dfrac{10}{18}=\dfrac{5}{9}$. **Answer: $5:9$.**

**Example 3 (fractions, then money).** A is $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of B and B is $\dfrac{5}{6}$ of C. Rs 5900 is shared among A, B, C in this ratio. Find each share.
- $A:B=3:4$ and $B:C=5:6$. LCM of 4 and 5 is 20.
- $A:B=15:20$ and $B:C=20:24$. So $A:B:C=15:20:24$.
- Parts $=59$, one part $=5900\div59=100$.
- **A = Rs 1500, B = Rs 2000, C = Rs 2400.**

**Your turn:** $A:B=3:4$ and $B:C=6:7$. Find $A:B:C$. (Answer: $9:12:14$. LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.)

### Type 5. Fourth, third and mean proportional

**How you spot it:** "Find the fourth proportional to 4, 6, 16", "third proportional to 4 and 6", "mean proportional between 4 and 9".

**Think:** each is just "product of extremes = product of means" in disguise.

| Name | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fourth proportional to $a,b,c$ | the number $x$ with $a:b=c:x$, so $x=\dfrac{bc}{a}$ |
| Third proportional to $a,b$ | the number $x$ with $a:b=b:x$, so $x=\dfrac{b^2}{a}$ |
| Mean proportional between $a,b$ | the number $x$ with $a:x=x:b$, so $x=\sqrt{ab}$ |

**Example 1.** Fourth proportional to 4, 6, 16.
- $4:6=16:x$, so $x=\dfrac{6\times16}{4}$ = **24**.

**Example 2.** Third proportional to 4 and 6.
- $x=\dfrac{6\times6}{4}$ = **9**.

**Example 3.** Mean proportional between 4 and 9.
- $x=\sqrt{4\times9}=\sqrt{36}$ = **6**.

**Example 4 (decimals).** Mean proportional between 0.4 and 0.1.
- $\sqrt{0.4\times0.1}=\sqrt{0.04}$ = **0.2**.

**Exam twist:** "5, $x$, 20 are in continued proportion" means $5:x=x:20$, so $x$ is the mean proportional: $x=\sqrt{100}=10$.

**Your turn:** Find the fourth proportional to 6, 9 and 14. (Answer: 21. $\dfrac{9\times14}{6}=21$.)

### Type 6. Duplicate, triplicate, sub-duplicate, sub-triplicate

**How you spot it:** these four words in the question.

**Think:** the name tells you the power.

| Name | What to do to $a:b$ | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicate | square both terms | $a^2:b^2$ |
| Triplicate | cube both terms | $a^3:b^3$ |
| Sub-duplicate | square root of both | $\sqrt{a}:\sqrt{b}$ |
| Sub-triplicate | cube root of both | $\sqrt[3]{a}:\sqrt[3]{b}$ |

**Example 1.** Duplicate of $3:4$ is $9:16$. Triplicate of $2:3$ is $8:27$.

**Example 2.** Sub-duplicate of $16:25$ is $4:5$. Sub-triplicate of $8:27$ is $2:3$.

**Example 3 (mix of all).** Find the compounded ratio of the duplicate of $3:4$ and the sub-duplicate of $16:25$.
- Duplicate of $3:4$ is $9:16$. Sub-duplicate of $16:25$ is $4:5$.
- Compounded: $\dfrac{9\times4}{16\times5}=\dfrac{36}{80}=\dfrac{9}{20}$.
- **Answer: $9:20$.**

**Your turn:** Triplicate of $3:5$? (Answer: $27:125$.)

### Type 7. Adding or subtracting the same number to both terms

**How you spot it:** "Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 8 is added to each, the ratio becomes 5 : 7."

**Think:** use the $k$ method. Write the numbers as $3k$ and $5k$, apply the change, set up a cross-multiplication.

**Example 1 (add).** Ratio $3:5$. Adding 8 to each gives $5:7$.
- $\dfrac{3k+8}{5k+8}=\dfrac{5}{7}$.
- Cross-multiply: $7(3k+8)=5(5k+8)$, so $21k+56=25k+40$.
- $16=4k$, so $k=4$.
- Numbers: $12$ and $20$. Check: $\dfrac{20}{28}=\dfrac{5}{7}$. **Answer: 12 and 20.**

**Example 2 (subtract, numbers given).** What must be subtracted from each of 19 and 25 so the ratio becomes $2:3$?
- $\dfrac{19-x}{25-x}=\dfrac{2}{3}$, so $3(19-x)=2(25-x)$.
- $57-3x=50-2x$, so $x$ = **7**. Check: $\dfrac{12}{18}=\dfrac{2}{3}$.

**Example 3 (only one term changes).** Two numbers are in ratio $7:5$. If 8 is added to the second only, the ratio becomes $7:6$. Find the numbers.
- $\dfrac{7k}{5k+8}=\dfrac{7}{6}$, so $42k=35k+56$ and $k=8$.
- **Answer: 56 and 40.**

**Your turn:** What number must be added to each of 9 and 13 to make the ratio $3:4$? (Answer: 3. $4(9+x)=3(13+x)$, so $x=3$.)

### Type 8. Ages

**How you spot it:** "Present ages are in the ratio... after 6 years..." or "6 years ago...".

**Think:** this is Type 7 with a story. The key fact: **everyone ages by the same number of years**, so each age goes up (or down) by the same amount.

**Example 1 (future).** Present ages of A and B are in the ratio $5:3$. After 6 years the ratio will be $7:5$. Find A's present age.
- $\dfrac{5k+6}{3k+6}=\dfrac{7}{5}$, so $25k+30=21k+42$ and $k=3$.
- A $=5\times3$ = **15 years**. (B $=9$.)

**Example 2 (father and son).** Ratio $7:2$ now. After 12 years it will be $9:4$. Find their ages.
- $\dfrac{7k+12}{2k+12}=\dfrac{9}{4}$, so $28k+48=18k+108$ and $k=6$.
- **Father 42, son 12.**

**Example 3 (past).** Six years ago A was 5 times as old as B. Their present ages are in the ratio $3:1$. Find A's present age.
- Present ages $3k$ and $k$. Six years ago: $3k-6=5(k-6)$.
- $3k-6=5k-30$, so $2k=24$ and $k=12$.
- A = **36 years**, B $=12$.

**Exam twist:** the **difference** of two ages never changes. If the question gives a sum or a difference, you can often go straight to one part without forming an equation.

**Your turn:** Ages are in ratio $3:5$. After 10 years the ratio will be $2:3$. Find the smaller present age. (Answer: 30. $\dfrac{3k+10}{5k+10}=\dfrac{2}{3}$ gives $k=10$.)

### Type 9. Income, expenditure and savings

**How you spot it:** "Incomes are in the ratio... expenditures in the ratio... each saves Rs..."

**Think:** **Income $-$ Expenditure = Savings.** Give each person's income and expenditure their own $k$.

**Example 1 (two persons).** Incomes of A and B are in the ratio $5:4$ and expenditures in the ratio $3:2$. Each saves Rs 1600. Find the incomes.
- Let incomes be $5x$, $4x$ and expenditures $3y$, $2y$.
- $5x-3y=1600$ and $4x-2y=1600$.
- Multiply the second by 1.5: $6x-3y=2400$. Subtract the first: $x=800$.
- Then $3y=5\times800-1600=2400$, so $y=800$.
- **A's income = Rs 4000, B's income = Rs 3200.**

**Example 2 (one person).** Income and expenditure are in the ratio $7:5$. Income is Rs 28000. Find the savings.
- Savings $=7-5=2$ parts. One part $=28000\div7=4000$.
- Savings = **Rs 8000**.

**Your turn:** Incomes are in ratio $6:5$, expenditures in ratio $4:3$, and each saves Rs 1000. Find A's income. (Answer: Rs 3000. $6x-4y=1000$ and $5x-3y=1000$ give $x=y=500$.)

### Type 10. Coins and notes

**How you spot it:** "A bag has Re 1, 50 paise and 25 paise coins in the ratio... total value..." or "amounts are in the ratio..."

**Think:** there are two different ratios hiding here: the ratio of **number of coins** and the ratio of **value**. Convert value to the same footing.

- Number $\times$ face value = value.
- If the **numbers** are in a ratio, multiply each part by the coin value to get the value ratio.
- If the **amounts** are in a ratio, divide each amount by the coin value to get the numbers.

**Example 1 (numbers given).** Numbers of Re 1, 50 p and 25 p coins are in the ratio $4:3:2$. Total value is Rs 150. How many 25 p coins?
- Values per $k$: $4\times1+3\times0.50+2\times0.25=4+1.5+0.5=\text{Rs }6$.
- $6k=150$, so $k=25$.
- 25 p coins $=2k$ = **50 coins**.

**Example 2 (amounts given).** Amounts in Re 1, 50 p and 25 p coins are in the ratio $5:3:2$ and the total is Rs 120. Find the number of each.
- Amounts: $60$, $36$, $24$ rupees.
- Coins: $60\div1=60$, $36\div0.5=72$, $24\div0.25=96$.
- **Answer: 60, 72 and 96 coins.**

**Your turn:** Numbers of Re 1, 50 p and 25 p coins are in the ratio $3:2:4$. Total Rs 150. How many 25 p coins? (Answer: 120. Value per $k=3+1+1=5$, so $k=30$.)

### Type 11. Percentages inside ratios

**How you spot it:** "A is 20% more than B", "20% of A equals 30% of B", "incomes rise by 20% and 10%".

**Think:** change every percentage into a ratio or a multiplier.

- 20% more than B means A $=1.2\,B$, so $A:B=6:5$.
- 25% less than C means B $=0.75\,C$, so $B:C=3:4$.

**Example 1 (chain).** A is 20% more than B. B is 25% less than C. Find $A:B:C$.
- $A:B=6:5$ and $B:C=3:4$. LCM of 5 and 3 is 15.
- $A:B=18:15$ and $B:C=15:20$.
- **$A:B:C=18:15:20$.**

**Example 2 (changed values).** Incomes are in the ratio $3:5$. The first rises by 20% and the second by 10%. New ratio?
- $\dfrac{3\times1.2}{5\times1.1}=\dfrac{3.6}{5.5}=\dfrac{36}{55}$.
- **Answer: $36:55$.**

**Example 3 (equal percentages).** If 20% of A = 30% of B, find $A:B$.
- $0.2A=0.3B$, so $\dfrac{A}{B}=\dfrac{0.3}{0.2}=\dfrac{3}{2}$.
- **Answer: $3:2$.** Note the swap: the smaller percentage goes with the bigger number.

**Exam twist:** if A is 25% more than B and B is 20% less than C, then $A:C=\dfrac{5}{4}\times\dfrac{4}{5}=1$. So A equals C. Do not assume they differ.

**Your turn:** If 40% of A = 25% of B, find $A:B$. (Answer: $5:8$. $\dfrac{A}{B}=\dfrac{25}{40}$.)

### Type 12. Sum of squares, product, or LCM given

**How you spot it:** "Two numbers are in the ratio 5 : 7 and the sum of their squares is 1850."

**Think:** write them as $ak$ and $bk$. The condition becomes an equation in $k^2$.

**Example 1 (sum of squares).** Ratio $5:7$, sum of squares 1850.
- $25k^2+49k^2=74k^2=1850$, so $k^2=25$ and $k=5$.
- **Numbers: 25 and 35.**

**Example 2 (product).** Ratio $3:4$, product 192.
- $12k^2=192$, so $k^2=16$ and $k=4$. **Numbers: 12 and 16.**

**Example 3 (LCM).** Two numbers in ratio $3:4$ have LCM 48.
- Write them as $3h$ and $4h$, where $h$ is their HCF. Their LCM is $12h$.
- $12h=48$ so $h=4$. **Numbers: 12 and 16.**

**Your turn:** Ratio $2:3$, sum of squares 468. Find the larger. (Answer: 18. $13k^2=468$, so $k=6$.)

### Type 13. Componendo and dividendo

**How you spot it:** "If $\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}=\dfrac{7}{3}$, find $a:b$", or "If $a:b=3:4$, find $\dfrac{2a+3b}{3a-b}$".

**Think:** the rule in one line: if $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{c}{d}$ then $\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}=\dfrac{c+d}{c-d}$. Read it forwards or backwards.

**Example 1 (forwards).** $a:b=5:2$. Find $\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}$.
- $\dfrac{5+2}{5-2}$ = **$\dfrac{7}{3}$**.

**Example 2 (backwards).** $\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}=\dfrac{7}{3}$. Find $a:b$.
- $\dfrac{a}{b}=\dfrac{7+3}{7-3}=\dfrac{10}{4}=\dfrac{5}{2}$. **Answer: $5:2$.**

**Example 3 (substitution).** $a:b=3:4$. Find $\dfrac{2a+3b}{3a-b}$.
- Take $a=3$, $b=4$: $\dfrac{6+12}{9-4}$ = **$\dfrac{18}{5}$**.

**Your turn:** $\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}=\dfrac{5}{3}$. Find $a:b$. (Answer: $4:1$.)

### Type 14. Direct and inverse proportion

**How you spot it:** "12 pens cost Rs 90, how much do 20 cost?", "6 men take 10 days, how long for 4 men?", "$x$ varies as the square of $y$".

**Think:** decide direction first. Ask yourself: if one doubles, does the other double (direct) or halve (inverse)?

**Example 1 (direct).** 12 pens cost Rs 90. Cost of 20 pens?
- One pen $=90\div12=7.5$. Twenty pens = **Rs 150**.

**Example 2 (inverse).** 6 men finish a job in 10 days. How many days for 4 men?
- Man-days $=6\times10=60$. Days $=60\div4$ = **15 days**.

**Example 3 (square).** $x$ varies directly as $y^2$. When $y=3$, $x=18$. Find $x$ when $y=5$.
- $\dfrac{x}{y^2}$ is constant: $\dfrac{18}{9}=2$. So $x=2\times25$ = **50**.

**Your turn:** 9 pumps empty a tank in 20 hours. How long will 15 pumps take? (Answer: 12 hours. $9\times20=180$, $180\div15$.)

## 4. One page to revise before the exam

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Divide in ratio | Add parts, find one part, multiply |
| Total or difference given | Match it with the parts it covers |
| $A:B$ and $B:C$ | Make B equal using the LCM |
| Compounded ratio | Multiply the tops, multiply the bottoms |
| Fourth proportional | $\dfrac{bc}{a}$ |
| Third proportional | $\dfrac{b^2}{a}$ |
| Mean proportional | $\sqrt{ab}$ |
| Duplicate, triplicate | Square, cube |
| Add same number to both | $\dfrac{ak+n}{bk+n}$, cross-multiply |
| Ages | Same years added to every age |
| Income, expenditure | Income $-$ Expenditure = Savings, two unknowns |
| Coins | Number $\times$ value, convert both ways |
| "x% more" | Multiplier $1+\frac{x}{100}$ |
| Sum of squares or product | Use $k^2$ |
| $\dfrac{a+b}{a-b}$ | Componendo and dividendo |
| Inverse proportion | Product stays constant |

**Memory aid for proportion words:** *Fourth* needs three numbers, *third* needs two, *mean* needs two and takes a root. The word "mean" always means square root.

## 5. Mistakes that cost marks

1. **Not converting units first.** 2 hours : 45 minutes is $8:3$, not $2:45$.
2. **Adding the same number to the ratio instead of the quantities.** In "add 8 to each number", the 8 goes on $3k$ and $5k$, not on 3 and 5.
3. **Using the wrong number of parts.** For a difference, use the difference in parts; for a total, use the sum of parts.
4. **Swapping the percentage ratio.** If 20% of A = 30% of B, the answer is $A:B=3:2$, not $2:3$.
5. **Forgetting to take the square root** after solving for $k^2$.
6. **Mixing coin numbers with coin values.** Check whether the question gives you counts or amounts.
7. **Chain ratios without a common middle.** $A:B=2:3$ and $B:C=4:5$ does not give $2:3:5$.
8. **Reversing direct and inverse.** More men means fewer days, but more pens means more cost.

## 6. Practice questions

Do these on paper. Do the first 10 untimed. Do the next 15 with 90 seconds each. Do the last 15 like an exam.

### Level 1: single idea

1. Simplify the ratio $144 : 216$.
2. Which is greater, $\dfrac{7}{11}$ or $\dfrac{9}{14}$?
3. Divide Rs 2400 in the ratio $3:5$. What is the larger share?
4. Find the fourth proportional to 9, 12 and 21.
5. Find the mean proportional between 9 and 49.
6. Divide Rs 7700 in the ratio $5:6$. What is the larger share?
7. Find the third proportional to 5 and 15.
8. Two numbers are in the ratio $7:4$ and differ by 54. Find the larger.
9. $A:B=2:3$ and $B:C=4:5$. Find $A:C$.
