# Pipes & Cisterns

> **SIGN RULE:** inlet = plus; outlet/leak = minus.

## Think about the tank each hour

A pipe's filling time tells you its rate: the fraction of a tank it adds in one hour. If two pipes are open, the tank receives both contributions each hour. A leak removes water, so subtract its rate—not its emptying time. Once the net fraction per hour is known, divide the fraction still needed by that rate.

### Walk through: an inlet fills in 8 hours; an outlet empties in 12

The inlet adds $1/8$ tank per hour; the outlet removes $1/12$. The net is $1/8-1/12=1/24$ tank per hour, so a full tank takes 24 hours.

**Sanity check:** The leak slows filling, so the combined time must be longer than 8 hours. **Your turn:** an inlet fills in 6 hours and a leak empties in 18. How long together? *(Answer: 9 hours; net rate $1/6-1/18=1/9$.)*

## ⚡ Formula cards

| Pipe | Rate |
|---|---|
| Inlet fills in $x$ hours | **$+\frac1x$ tank/hour** |
| Outlet empties in $y$ hours | **$-\frac1y$ tank/hour** |
| Several pipes together | **Add signed rates** |
| Time to fill | **$\frac1{\text{net rate}}$**, if net rate $>0$ |

> **Picture the tank:** Filling is **+**; draining is **−**. Add the signs before taking the reciprocal.

```text
 INLET (+) ──┐
             ├──> NET RATE ──> flip ──> FILL TIME
OUTLET (-) ──┘
```

## Exam shortcut playbook

| If you spot... | Shortcut |
|---|---|
| Two or more inlets | Add fractions of a tank filled per hour. |
| A leak or outlet | Subtract its rate; never subtract filling times. |
| A known combined time and one pipe time | Subtract rates to isolate the missing pipe. |
| A tank partly full | Divide only the remaining fraction by the net rate. |
| Litres per minute and tank capacity | Work directly in litres per minute; no need to convert to tank fractions. |

## Ten worked examples

### 1. Two inlets together
**Question:** Pipes fill a tank in 12 and 18 hours. Find their combined time.
- **Step by step:** Their hourly rates are $1/12$ and $1/18$. Together: $5/36$ tank/hour.
- **Fast way:** Take 36 tank units: pipes add 3 and 2 units per hour. **Answer: $36/5=7.2$ hours.**

### 2. Inlet and outlet together
**Question:** An inlet fills in 8 hours; an outlet empties in 12. Find the time with both open.
- **Step by step:** Net rate $=1/8-1/12=1/24$ tank/hour.
- **Fast way:** In 24 units, the inlet adds 3 and outlet removes 2 each hour. **Answer: $24$ hours.**

### 3. Find the leak rate
**Question:** An inlet fills in 6 hours; with a leak, it takes 10 hours. How long would the leak take to empty the tank?
- **Step by step:** Leak rate $=1/6-1/10=1/15$ tank/hour.
- **Fast way:** The leak removes one-fifteenth of a tank per hour. **Answer: $15$ hours.**

### 4. Pipes alternate hours
**Question:** Pipes fill in 4 and 6 hours. They run one hour each, first the 4-hour pipe. Find the fill time.
- **Step by step:** A two-hour cycle fills $1/4+1/6=5/12$. After 4 hours, $1/6$ remains; the first pipe fills it in $2/3$ hour.
- **Fast way:** Two full cycles take 4 hours; finish the last sixth on pipe 1. **Answer: $4\frac23$ hours.**

### 5. Find the outlet time
**Question:** A pipe fills in 5 hours, but with a leak it takes 6. Find the leak's emptying time.
- **Step by step:** Leak rate $=1/5-1/6=1/30$ tank/hour.
- **Fast way:** Reciprocal of the leak rate is 30 hours. **Answer: $30$ hours.**

### 6. Use three pipes together
**Question:** Two inlets fill a tank in 6 and 8 hours. An outlet empties it in 12 hours. How long do all three take together?
- **Step by step:** Net rate $=\frac{1}{6}+\frac{1}{8}-\frac{1}{12}=\frac{5}{24}$ tank per hour.
- **Calculation:** Time $=\frac{24}{5}$. **Answer: $4.8$ hours.**

### 7. Find work completed in part of an hour
**Question:** A pipe fills a tank in 9 hours. What fraction does it fill in 4 hours?
- **Step by step:** Multiply its hourly rate by the elapsed time.
- **Calculation:** $4(\frac{1}{9})=\frac{4}{9}$. **Answer: $\frac{4}{9}$ of the tank.**

### 8. Find the time for a partly filled tank
**Question:** A tank is half full. An inlet fills it in 8 hours and an outlet empties it in 24 hours. How long until it is full with both open?
- **Shortcut:** Net rate $=1/8-1/24=1/12$ tank/hour. Only half the tank remains.
- **Calculation:** $(1/2)/(1/12)$. **Answer: $6$ hours.**

### 9. Work directly in litres
**Question:** A pump adds 30 L per minute to a 900 L tank. How long to fill it?
- **Shortcut:** Keep units as litres; time $=\text{capacity}/\text{rate}$.
- **Calculation:** $900/30$. **Answer: $30$ minutes.**

### 10. Find an outlet from the combined time
**Question:** An inlet fills a tank in 10 hours. With an outlet open, the tank fills in 15 hours. How long would the outlet alone take to empty it?
- **Shortcut:** Outlet rate $=1/10-1/15=1/30$ tank/hour.
- **Answer:** **30 hours** to empty the tank.

## Quick practice

1. Two inlets fill a tank in 4 and 12 hours. How long together? *(Answer: 3 hours; rate $1/4+1/12=1/3$.)*
2. A pipe fills $2/5$ of a tank in 4 hours. How long for a full tank at the same rate? *(Answer: 10 hours; rate is $1/10$ tank per hour.)*
3. A tank is one-quarter full; a pipe fills 1/8 tank per hour. How long to fill it? *(Answer: 6 hours; remaining $3/4$ divided by $1/8$.)*
