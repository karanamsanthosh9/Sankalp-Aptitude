# Ratio & Proportion

## 1. Core Concept & Fundamental Equations

- A ratio $a:b$ compares quantities in the same units; it is equivalent to $a/b$.
- If $A:B=m:n$ and $A+B=S$, then $A=\frac{m}{m+n}S$ and $B=\frac{n}{m+n}S$.
- A proportion $a:b=c:d$ means $ad=bc$.
- Direct variation: $y=kx$. Inverse variation: $y=k/x$; the product $xy$ remains constant.
- To combine $A:B$ and $B:C$, make the two $B$ terms equal, then read the combined ratio.
- Partnership profit is proportional to capital multiplied by investment time.

## 2. Universal Shortcut Engine

| Technique | Use it when | Avoid it when |
|---|---|---|
| Parts method | A total is divided in a known ratio. | Conditions include changing quantities; form equations instead. |
| Equalize the common term | Two ratios share a middle quantity. | The shared quantity is not the same measure or time. |
| Scale factor | Quantities vary directly or inversely. | The relationship is not stated or implied to be proportional. |
| Capital-time product | Partnership investments change over time. | Profit has a fixed salary/commission before division. |
| Cross multiplication | A proportion has one unknown. | Units are inconsistent; convert them first. |

## 3. Model-Wise Question Classification

### Model 1: Divide a total in a ratio
**Problem:** Divide $840$ in the ratio $3:4$.
- **Method A:** Total parts $=7$; one part $=840/7=120$. Shares are $360$ and $480$.
- **Method B:** Shares are $3/7$ and $4/7$ of $840$: $360$ and $480$. **Answer: $360,480$.**

### Model 2: Combine two ratios
**Problem:** If $A:B=2:3$ and $B:C=4:5$, find $A:B:C$.
- **Method A:** Make $B$ equal to $12$: scale first ratio by $4$ to get $8:12$; second by $3$ to get $12:15$. Combined ratio $8:12:15$.
- **Method B:** LCM of the middle terms $3$ and $4$ is $12$; scale and join. **Answer: $8:12:15$.**

### Model 3: Direct variation
**Problem:** If $5$ notebooks cost $150$, what do $8$ notebooks cost at the same rate?
- **Method A:** Unit cost $=150/5=30$; total $=8\times30=240$.
- **Method B:** Cost varies directly: $150(8/5)=240$. **Answer: $240$.**

### Model 4: Inverse variation
**Problem:** 12 workers finish a job in 15 days. How many days will 20 workers take at the same rate?
- **Method A:** Total work $=12\times15=180$ worker-days; days $=180/20=9$.
- **Method B:** Workers and days are inversely proportional: $12\times15=20d$, so $d=9$. **Answer: $9$ days.**

### Model 5: Partnership
**Problem:** A invests $6000$ for 12 months and B invests $8000$ for 9 months. Divide profit $10,000$.
- **Method A:** Capital-time products are $72,000$ and $72,000$, so split equally: $5000$ each.
- **Method B:** Profit ratio $=6000(12):8000(9)=1:1$. **Answer: $5000$ each.**

## 4. Exam-Specific Alignment & Variations

- **SSC lens:** Ratios combined with ages, mixtures, percentage changes, and redistribution of money.
- **RRB lens:** Direct/inverse proportion, workers and days, speed and time, and quick sharing questions.
- **AP State / PSU lens:** Partnership, changing ratios, proportional production, and multi-condition word problems.

## 5. Common Traps & Speed Killers

1. **Adding ratio terms without aligning the common quantity:** equalize the middle term before combining ratios.
2. **Using direct variation for a fixed job:** workers and time are inverse when work and efficiency are fixed.
3. **Ignoring investment duration:** partnership ratio is capital times time, not capital alone.
