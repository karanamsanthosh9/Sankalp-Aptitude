# Percentages

> Read this like a class, not like a formula sheet. Every idea comes with a picture in your head, then a solved example, then one for you to try on paper. Do the "Your turn" problems before reading the answer.

## 0. Start here: what is this topic really about?

Imagine a fruit seller who says "I have sold 35 out of every 100 mangoes." That single idea - a number expressed as a part of 100 - is a percentage. The word literally comes from Latin: **per centum**, meaning "for every hundred."

Percentages are everywhere in the paper:

- A student scored 72 marks out of 80. What is the percentage?
- Milk price rose 25%. By how much should a family cut consumption to keep spending the same?
- A salary went up 10%, then down 10%. Is the net change zero?
- In an election, a candidate won by 2400 votes. Total votes cast?
- A mixture of acid and water - how much water to dilute it?

**What the exam gives you:** a base number and a percent, or two numbers and a question about their ratio, or a chain of percent changes.

**What it asks for:** a resulting number, an original number, a percent change, or how two quantities compare after changes.

**How many marks to expect:** SSC CGL carries 3 to 5 questions directly on percentages. RRB NTPC carries 2 to 3. IBPS and SBI PO carry 4 to 6 (many others - like Profit/Loss and SI/CI - depend on percentage skill). APPSC and PSU carry 2 to 4. This is one of the five most tested topics in every exam.

## 1. The only idea you must understand

### Percent means "out of 100"

$x\%$ of a number $N$ means $\dfrac{x}{100}\times N$.

That is the whole idea. Everything else follows from it.

$$35\%\ \text{of}\ 480 = \dfrac{35}{100}\times480 = 168$$

**Finding what percent one number is of another:**

$$A\ \text{is what \% of}\ B?\ \Rightarrow\ \dfrac{A}{B}\times100$$

Example: 45 is what percent of 180? $\dfrac{45}{180}\times100 = 25\%$.

### Percent increase and decrease

**Increase by $p\%$:** multiply by $\left(1+\dfrac{p}{100}\right)$, which is the same as $\dfrac{100+p}{100}$.

**Decrease by $p\%$:** multiply by $\left(1-\dfrac{p}{100}\right)$, which is $\dfrac{100-p}{100}$.

$$\text{300 decreased by 20\%} = 300\times\dfrac{80}{100} = 240$$

### Finding the original number

If after a $p\%$ increase the number is $N$, the original was:

$$\text{Original} = \dfrac{N\times100}{100+p}$$

If after a $p\%$ decrease it is $N$:

$$\text{Original} = \dfrac{N\times100}{100-p}$$

**Example:** after a 20% increase, a number is 360. Original $= 360\times\dfrac{100}{120} = 300$.

**Sense check:** if it went up, the original must be smaller than the result. If it went down, the original must be larger.

## 2. The shortcuts / tricks

### Trick 1: the two-way percent flip

If A is $x\%$ more than B, then B is less than A by exactly $\dfrac{x}{100+x}\times100\%$.

If A is 25% more than B, B is $\dfrac{25}{125}\times100 = 20\%$ less than A.

**Why it works:** A $= 1.25$B, so B $= \dfrac{A}{1.25} = 0.8A$. That is 20% less than A.

### Trick 2: successive percent changes

Two changes of $a\%$ and $b\%$ (use negative for decrease) give a net change of:

$$\text{Net \%} = a + b + \dfrac{ab}{100}$$

**Example:** 20% increase then 10% decrease.

$20 + (-10) + \dfrac{20\times(-10)}{100} = 10 - 2 = \mathbf{8\%}$ net increase.

**Example:** 25% increase then 20% decrease.

$25 + (-20) + \dfrac{25\times(-20)}{100} = 5 - 5 = \mathbf{0\%}$ net change.

### Trick 3: price-consumption trick

If price rises by $x\%$ and you want the total expenditure to stay the same, reduce consumption by:

$$\dfrac{x}{100+x}\times100\%$$

If price rises 25%, reduce consumption by $\dfrac{25}{125}\times100 = 20\%$.

**Why it works:** price $\times$ consumption = expenditure. If price becomes $\dfrac{125}{100}$ times, consumption must become $\dfrac{100}{125}$ times, which is $\dfrac{1}{1.25}=0.8$, a 20% reduction.

## 3. Types of questions and how to crack each one

### Type 1. Find $x\%$ of a number

**How you spot it:** "What is 35% of 480?" or "Find 12.5% of 640."

**Think:** multiply by the fraction form of the percent.

**Example:** $15\%$ of $560 = \dfrac{15}{100}\times560 = \mathbf{84}$.

**Exam twist:** use fractions for "awkward" percents. $12.5\% = \dfrac{1}{8}$. $16.67\%=\dfrac{1}{6}$. $33.33\%=\dfrac{1}{3}$.

**Your turn:** What is $37.5\%$ of $960$? (Answer: $\dfrac{3}{8}\times960=360$)

### Type 2. What percent is A of B?

**How you spot it:** "45 is what percent of 180?" or "63 is what percent of 84?"

**Think:** $\dfrac{A}{B}\times100$.

**Example:** $63$ is what percent of $84$? $= \dfrac{63}{84}\times100 = \dfrac{3}{4}\times100 = \mathbf{75\%}$.

**Your turn:** 18 is what percent of 120? (Answer: $\dfrac{18}{120}\times100=15\%$)

### Type 3. Percent increase or decrease

**How you spot it:** "A number is increased by 15%," "price falls by 20%."

**Think:** multiply the original by $(1+p/100)$ or $(1-p/100)$.

**Example 1:** 480 increased by $12.5\%$. $480\times\dfrac{9}{8} = \mathbf{540}$.

**Example 2:** income Rs 10000, spends 80%, saves? Saves $20\%$ of Rs 10000 = **Rs 2000**.

**Your turn:** 600 decreased by $16.\overline{6}\%$. (Answer: $600\times\dfrac{5}{6}=500$)

### Type 4. Find the original number

**How you spot it:** "After a 20% increase, the number is 540. What was the original?"

**Think:** the "after" number corresponds to $100+p$. Set up the proportion.

**Example 1:** After $20\%$ increase, a number is $540$. Original $= 540\div1.2 = \mathbf{450}$.

**Example 2:** After $25\%$ decrease, a number is $450$. Original $= 450\div0.75 = \mathbf{600}$.

**Your turn:** After a 15% decrease, a number is 340. Original? (Answer: $340\div0.85=400$)

### Type 5. Price change, consumption change

**How you spot it:** price of an item rises/falls; find the change in consumption needed to keep spending constant.

**Think:** use $\dfrac{x}{100+x}\times100$ for a price rise of $x\%$.

**Example:** price of rice rises $25\%$. By what percent should a family reduce consumption to maintain the same monthly expenditure?

$\dfrac{25}{125}\times100 = \mathbf{20\%}$.

**Exam twist:** if price **falls** by $x\%$, they can increase consumption by $\dfrac{x}{100-x}\times100\%$.

**Your turn:** price rises $20\%$. Reduce consumption by what percent? (Answer: $\dfrac{20}{120}\times100=16.\overline{6}\%$)

### Type 6. Election and voting questions

**How you spot it:** "Candidate A gets 55% of valid votes and wins by 2000 votes."

**Think:** the margin in votes equals the difference in percentages applied to the total.

**Example:** A gets 55%, B gets 45%. Margin $= 10\%$ of total $= 2000$. Total $= \mathbf{20000}$.

**Exam twist:** sometimes invalid votes are mentioned. Subtract them from total first to get valid votes.

**Your turn:** A gets 60%, B gets 40%. B loses by 2000 votes. Total votes? (Answer: $20\%$ of total $=2000$, total $=10000$)

### Type 7. Pass-fail questions

**How you spot it:** "75% of students passed. 60 students failed. How many appeared?"

**Think:** if pass% is given and the number of failures is given, work from $100\% - \text{pass}\% = \text{fail}\%$.

**Example 1:** $75\%$ pass, $60$ fail. Fail $= 25\%$ of total. Total $= 60\div0.25 = \mathbf{240}$.

**Example 2:** 240 students pass, which is 80% of total. Total $= 240\div0.8 = 300$. Failed $= \mathbf{60}$.

**Exam twist:** some questions give both pass and fail marks and ask for the aggregate. That is a different question - use the marks to find total.

**Your turn:** 80% students passed. 70 failed. How many appeared? (Answer: fail $=20\%$, total $=70\div0.2=350$)

### Type 8. Population increase/decrease

**How you spot it:** "population of 50000 grows at 10% per year for 2 years."

**Think:** apply the multiplier for each year one by one, like compound growth.

**Example:** population $50000$, grows $10\%$ per year for 2 years.

Year 1: $50000\times1.1 = 55000$. Year 2: $55000\times1.1 = \mathbf{60500}$.

**Example:** population 80000 falls $5\%$ per year. After 2 years? $80000\times0.95\times0.95 = \mathbf{72200}$.

**Your turn:** population 40000 grows 5%, then 10%. Final? (Answer: $40000\times1.05\times1.10=46200$)

### Type 9. Successive percent changes

**How you spot it:** "price increases 10%, then increases 20%", "salary up 10% then down 10%".

**Think:** multiply the two multipliers. Or use the formula $a+b+ab/100$.

**Example:** salary increased 10%, then decreased 10%. Net change?

$1.1\times0.9 = 0.99$. Net $= -1\%$. (Or formula: $10-10+\dfrac{10\times(-10)}{100}=-1\%$.)

**Example:** successive increases of $10\%$ and $20\%$. Net $= 10+20+\dfrac{10\times20}{100} = 32\%$.

**Your turn:** Two successive increases of 20% and 25%. Net percent increase? (Answer: $20+25+5=50\%$)

### Type 10. Percent more / percent less

**How you spot it:** "A's salary is 30% more than B's. B's salary is what percent less than A's?"

**Think:** "more than" is always calculated on the **base** (B). "Less than" must be calculated on the **new base** (A).

**Example:** A is $30\%$ more than B. B is $\dfrac{30}{130}\times100 = \mathbf{23.08\%}$ less than A.

**Your turn:** A's height is 40% more than B's. B's height is what percent less than A's? (Answer: $\dfrac{40}{140}\times100=28.57\%$)

### Type 11. Mixture and dilution

**How you spot it:** "a 40L mixture is 30% acid. How much water must be added to make it 20% acid?"

**Think:** the quantity of acid does not change, only the total volume changes.

**Acid stays the same:** $\text{old \%}\times\text{old volume} = \text{new \%}\times\text{new volume}$.

**Example:** 60L of milk is 80% pure milk. How much water to add to make it 60% pure?

Pure milk $= 60\times0.8 = 48L$. New total with $x$ litres of water: $\dfrac{48}{60+x}=0.60$.

$48 = 36+0.6x \implies 0.6x=12 \implies x = \mathbf{20L}$.

**Your turn:** 50L mixture is 40% spirit. How much water to make it 25% spirit? (Answer: spirit $=20L$. $\dfrac{20}{50+x}=0.25 \implies 20=12.5+0.25x \implies x=30L$)

### Type 12. Income, expenditure and savings

**How you spot it:** salary Rs 12000, spends 70% on household, 15% on clothes. Save?

**Think:** savings = income $-$ expenditure. If spending fractions are given, sum them and subtract from 100%.

**Example:** income Rs 15000, spends 60% on household, 15% on clothes. Saves?

Remaining $= 100-60-15 = 25\%$. Savings $= 15000\times0.25 = \mathbf{Rs\ 3750}$.

**Exam twist:** "income increases 20%, expenditure increases 10%, find percentage change in savings." Set up with actual numbers: old income I, old expenditure E, old savings S = I - E. New savings = 1.2I - 1.1E. Compute change.

**Your turn:** income Rs 8000, spends 75%. Saves? (Answer: $8000\times0.25=Rs\ 2000$)

### Type 13. Ratio from percentage condition

**How you spot it:** "20% of A = 30% of B. Find A:B."

**Think:** write the equation and solve.

$\dfrac{20}{100}\times A = \dfrac{30}{100}\times B \implies \dfrac{A}{B} = \dfrac{30}{20} = \dfrac{3}{2}$. So A:B $= 3:2$.

**Your turn:** 15% of A = 25% of B. Find A:B. (Answer: $\dfrac{A}{B}=\dfrac{25}{15}=\dfrac{5}{3}$, so $5:3$)

## 4. One page to revise before the exam

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| $x\%$ of a number | Multiply number by $x/100$ |
| A is what \% of B | $(A/B)\times100$ |
| Increase by $p\%$ | Multiply by $(100+p)/100$ |
| Decrease by $p\%$ | Multiply by $(100-p)/100$ |
| Find original after increase $p\%$ | Divide result by $(100+p)/100$ |
| Find original after decrease $p\%$ | Divide result by $(100-p)/100$ |
| A is $x\%$ more than B, B is ?\% less | $x/(100+x)\times100$ |
| Successive changes $a\%$ and $b\%$ | $a+b+ab/100$ |
| Price up $x\%$, keep spend same | Reduce consumption by $x/(100+x)\times100\%$ |
| Election: win by $N$ votes, difference $d\%$ | Total votes $= N/d\times100$ |
| Pass\%, fail number given | Total = fail number $\div$ fail\% |
| Population year on year | Multiply by factor for each year |
| Mixture dilution | Quantity of solute stays constant |
| Ratio from percent condition | Write equation, cross-multiply |

## 5. Mistakes that cost marks

1. **"More than" versus "more":** "A is 25 more than B" and "A is 25% more than B" are completely different. Read carefully.
2. **Wrong base for percent:** profit%, loss%, increase% - all are calculated on the **original** (base) value, not the new value.
3. **Successive changes are not additive:** up 10% then down 10% is NOT zero change. It is -1%.
4. **Confusing "percent of" with "percent more":** 120% of B is not the same as 20% more than B - wait, it is. But 120% of B is NOT B increased by 120%; the latter would be 220% of B.
5. **Price-consumption trap:** "price rises by 1/4, reduce consumption by 1/4" is wrong. Reduce by 1/5 (use the formula).
6. **Population over multiple years:** each year applies the rate to the new (increased) population, not the original.
7. **Election: "got 55% of votes"** means the margin is $(55-45)\% = 10\%$, not 55%.
8. **Mixture: changing the solvent, not the solute.** When water is added, the acid quantity does not change - only the total volume does.
