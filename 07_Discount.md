# Discount

> Read this like a class, not like a formula list. Every idea comes with a picture in your head, then a solved example, then one for you to try. Do the "Your turn" boxes with a pen before reading the answer next to them.

## 0. Start here: what is this topic really about?

Picture a shop window with a big board: **"SALE: 20% OFF on everything!"** A shirt has a tag that says Rs 1000. You do not pay Rs 1000. You pay Rs 800. The Rs 1000 on the tag is the **marked price**, the Rs 200 the shop forgives is the **discount**, and the Rs 800 you actually hand over is the **selling price**.

That is the whole chapter. Questions simply ask you to move between these three numbers, again and again, sometimes with the shopkeeper's cost thrown in. They look like this:

- "A shirt marked at Rs 2500 is sold at 12% discount. Find the selling price."
- "Two successive discounts of 20% and 10%. What single discount is the same?"
- "A trader marks goods 30% above cost and gives 10% discount. Profit or loss?"
- "Buy 4 get 1 free. What is the discount percentage?"

**What the exam gives you:** a marked price, a selling price, a discount rate (or two or three of them), and sometimes the cost price or a profit percentage. **What it asks for:** a selling price, a discount percentage, a marked price, or a profit or loss percentage.

**How many marks to expect:** SSC CGL and CHSL usually carry 1 to 3 questions that need discount (often mixed with Profit and Loss). RRB NTPC and Group D carry 1 or 2. IBPS and SBI Clerk and PO papers carry 1 or 2 inside the arithmetic or data sets. PSU and APPSC papers carry 1 or 2. This chapter is a gift: the ideas are small, and every question follows one of the patterns below.

## 1. The only ideas you must understand

### Marked price, discount, selling price

- **Marked price (MP)** is the price printed on the tag. It is also called list price.
- **Discount** is the amount the shop takes off the marked price.
- **Selling price (SP)** is what the customer really pays.

$$\text{Discount} = \text{MP} - \text{SP}$$

The most important sentence of the chapter: **discount percentage is always worked out on the marked price**, never on the selling price.

$$\text{Discount \%} = \dfrac{\text{Discount}}{\text{MP}}\times 100$$

### What "d% discount" really means

A 20% discount means 20 parts of every 100 are forgiven. So you pay the other 80 parts.

- Take MP $=100$. Discount $=20$. You pay $80$.
- So SP is **80% of MP**, that is, $\text{SP}=\text{MP}\times\dfrac{100-d}{100}$.

### Trying it: MP is Rs 1500 and the discount is 20%. Find SP.

Step 1. Discount $=20\%$ of $1500=300$.

Step 2. SP $=1500-300=1200$.

Or directly: you pay 80% of 1500, which is $1500\times0.8=1200$.

**Answer: Rs 1200.** Both ways agree.

**Sense check you should always do:** the selling price must be **less** than the marked price. A discount percentage must be **below 100**. If your answer breaks either rule, you have swapped something.

### Discount is on MP, profit is on CP

The shopkeeper has a **cost price (CP)**, what he paid. Discount is measured against the marked price. Profit and loss are measured against the cost price. Two different bases. Half the mistakes in this chapter come from mixing them.

### Your turn

MP is Rs 800 and the discount is 25%. Find SP. (Answer: Rs 600. Discount is 200, and $800-200=600$.)

## 2. The shortcuts that save time

### Shortcut 1. The multiplier

Instead of "find discount, then subtract", go straight to what you **pay**.

| Discount | You pay | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 90% | 0.9 |
| 20% | 80% | 0.8 |
| 25% | 75% | 0.75 |
| 30% | 70% | 0.7 |
| 12.5% | 87.5% | 0.875 |

**Why it works:** SP is the part of MP that is left. Multiply once and you are done.

**Example.** A bag has MP Rs 2400 and a 15% discount. You pay $85\%$ of 2400 $=2400\times0.85=$ **Rs 2040**.

### Shortcut 2. Successive discounts multiply

If a shop gives 20% off and then another 10% off, the second 10% is taken on the **reduced** price. So the multipliers multiply:

$$0.8\times0.9=0.72$$

You pay 72%, so the net discount is $100-72=28\%$.

**Quick formula for two discounts $a\%$ and $b\%$:**

$$\text{Net discount} = a+b-\dfrac{ab}{100}$$

Check: $20+10-\dfrac{20\times10}{100}=30-2=28$. Same answer.

**Three discounts:** apply the formula twice. Take the first two to get one net discount, then combine that with the third.

**Example.** Discounts of 10%, 20% and 25%.
- First two: $10+20-2=28\%$.
- With the third: $28+25-\dfrac{28\times25}{100}=53-7=46\%$.
- Check by multipliers: $0.9\times0.8\times0.75=0.54$. You pay 54%, net discount **46%**.

The order does not matter. Any order gives the same net discount.

### Shortcut 3. Percent to fraction

Discounts like "one-eighth off" show up in questions that avoid percent signs. Know these cold.

| Fraction | Percent | You pay |
|---|---|---|
| $\frac{1}{2}$ | 50% | $\frac{1}{2}$ |
| $\frac{1}{4}$ | 25% | $\frac{3}{4}$ |
| $\frac{1}{5}$ | 20% | $\frac{4}{5}$ |
| $\frac{1}{8}$ | 12.5% | $\frac{7}{8}$ |
| $\frac{1}{10}$ | 10% | $\frac{9}{10}$ |
| $\frac{1}{20}$ | 5% | $\frac{19}{20}$ |

### Shortcut 4. The MP : SP ratio

A 20% discount means MP : SP $=100:80=5:4$. For any discount $d\%$, MP : SP $=100:(100-d)$.

**Why use it:** you can find MP from SP or SP from MP without a calculator, by splitting into units.

**Example.** SP is Rs 1360 after a 15% discount. MP : SP $=100:85=20:17$.
- 17 units $=1360$, so 1 unit $=80$.
- MP $=20\times80=$ **Rs 1600**.

## 3. Types of questions and how to crack each one

Every type you can meet is below. Each has: how you spot it, how to think, an example, and an exam twist where useful.

### Type 1. Direct: find the discount and the selling price

**How you spot it:** "An article marked at Rs 2500 is sold at a discount of 12%. Find the discount and the selling price."

**Think:** discount is a percentage of the marked price. Then SP is MP minus discount.

**Example 1.** MP Rs 2500, discount 12%.
- Discount $=\dfrac{12}{100}\times2500=300$.
- SP $=2500-300=$ **Rs 2200**.

**Example 2.** MP Rs 840, discount 25%.
- You pay 75%: $840\times0.75=$ **Rs 630**.

**Exam twist:** sometimes only the discount amount is asked. Do not waste time finding SP.

**Your turn:** MP Rs 1800, discount 35%. SP? (Answer: Rs 1170. You pay 65%: $1800\times0.65$.)

### Type 2. Finding the discount percentage

**How you spot it:** "An item marked at Rs 750 is sold for Rs 600. Find the discount percentage."

**Think:** first find the discount in rupees. Then divide by **MP** and multiply by 100.

**Example 1.** MP Rs 750, SP Rs 600.
- Discount $=750-600=150$.
- Discount $\%=\dfrac{150}{750}\times100=$ **20%**.

**Example 2.** MP Rs 960, SP Rs 840.
- Discount $=120$.
- $\dfrac{120}{960}\times100=$ **12.5%**.

**Exam twist:** if you divide by SP you get a wrong answer that is usually sitting in the options. In Example 1, dividing by SP gives $\dfrac{150}{600}=25\%$. That is the trap option.

**Your turn:** MP Rs 480, SP Rs 408. Discount percentage? (Answer: 15%. Discount is 72, and $\dfrac{72}{480}=0.15$.)

### Type 3. Finding the marked price from the selling price

**How you spot it:** "After a 15% discount, a shirt was sold for Rs 1530. What was its marked price?"

**Think:** SP is a fixed percent of MP, so MP is SP divided by that percent. A 15% discount means SP is 85% of MP.

$$\text{MP}=\dfrac{\text{SP}}{1-\text{discount rate}}$$

**Example 1.** SP Rs 1530 after 15% discount.
- $\text{MP}=\dfrac{1530}{0.85}=$ **Rs 1800**.

**Example 2.** SP Rs 2340 after 22% discount.
- $\text{MP}=\dfrac{2340}{0.78}=$ **Rs 3000**.

**Exam twist:** the discount amount may be given instead. "A discount of Rs 450 is 15% of the marked price. Find the SP."
- MP $=\dfrac{450}{0.15}=3000$. SP $=3000-450=$ **Rs 2550**.

**Your turn:** SP Rs 1260 after a 30% discount. MP? (Answer: Rs 1800. $\dfrac{1260}{0.7}$.)

### Type 4. Successive discounts, net discount

**How you spot it:** "Two successive discounts of 20% and 10%", "a discount of 20% followed by 10%".

**Think:** multiply the "you pay" fractions. Never add the discounts.

**Example 1.** Net discount for 20% and 10%.
- $20+10-\dfrac{20\times10}{100}=$ **28%**.

**Example 2.** Net discount for 10%, 20% and 25%.
- From Shortcut 2: **46%**.

**Example 3.** A table is marked at Rs 5000, with discounts of 20% and 10%. What does the buyer pay, and how much is saved?
- $5000\times0.8\times0.9=$ **Rs 3600**.
- Saved $=5000-3600=$ **Rs 1400**.

**Exam twist:** "Is 20% followed by 10% the same as a single 30% discount?" No. It is 28%. The difference is the small product term, $\dfrac{ab}{100}$.

**Your turn:** successive discounts of 25% and 20%. Net discount? (Answer: 40%. $25+20-5$.)

### Type 5. Comparing two offers

**How you spot it:** "Which is better for the customer: a single 30% discount, or two successive discounts of 20% and 10%?" Or "Find the difference in the amount paid under two schemes."

**Think:** turn every offer into a single net discount. The bigger net discount is better for the buyer.

**Example 1.** Single 30% against 20% followed by 10%.
- 20% and 10% is 28%.
- 30% is bigger, so the **single 30% is better**.

**Example 2.** On goods of Rs 8000, Scheme A is 25% off. Scheme B is 15% followed by 10% off. How much does the buyer save by picking the better one?
- Scheme A: 25%.
- Scheme B: $15+10-1.5=23.5\%$.
- A is better. On Rs 8000: A saves $0.25\times8000=2000$. B saves $0.235\times8000=1880$.
- Difference $=$ **Rs 120**.

**Your turn:** On a Rs 5000 item, which is better for the buyer: 25% off, or 20% followed by 8% off? By how much in rupees? (Answer: the second, by Rs 70. Net of the second is $20+8-1.6=26.4\%$, which is 1.4% more than 25%. 1.4% of 5000 is 70.)

### Type 6. A percentage discount plus a flat amount off

**How you spot it:** "A shop gives 10% discount and then a coupon worth Rs 200. What does the customer pay?"

**Think:** a percentage and a rupee amount do **not** commute. The order changes the answer. Read which comes first.

**Example 1.** MP Rs 2000. Discount 10% and a coupon of Rs 200.
- Percent first, then coupon: $2000\times0.9=1800$, then $1800-200=$ **Rs 1600**.
- Coupon first, then percent: $2000-200=1800$, then $1800\times0.9=$ **Rs 1620**.
- The buyer is better off with percent first, by Rs 20.

**Example 2.** A bag marked Rs 2400 has 25% off. A coupon then gives Rs 150 off the discounted price. What is paid?
- $2400\times0.75=1800$. Then $1800-150=$ **Rs 1650**.

**Your turn:** MP Rs 1500, discount 20%, then a coupon of Rs 100 off the discounted price. Pay? (Answer: Rs 1100. $1500\times0.8=1200$, and $1200-100=1100$.)

### Type 7. Marked above cost: profit or loss after discount

**How you spot it:** "A trader marks his goods 30% above cost and gives a discount of 10%. Find his profit or loss percentage."

**Think:** the cost price is the base for profit. So **take CP as 100**. Mark up from there, then take off the discount from the marked price.

**Example 1.** Marked 30% above CP, discount 10%.
- CP $=100$. MP $=130$.
- SP $=130\times0.9=117$.
- Profit $=17$, which is on 100. So **17% profit**.

**Example 2.** Marked 20% above CP, discount 25%.
- CP $=100$. MP $=120$. SP $=120\times0.75=90$.
- **10% loss.**

**Example 3.** A trader's goods cost Rs 1000 and are marked Rs 1250. He gives an 8% discount.
- SP $=1250\times0.92=1150$.
- Profit $=150$ on cost 1000, so **15%**.

**Exam twist:** the common error is to say "30% above, less 10%, equals 20% profit". That subtracts percentages with different bases. Always multiply.

**Your turn:** marked 40% above CP, discount 25%. Profit or loss? (Answer: 5% profit. $140\times0.75=105$.)

### Type 8. Finding the mark-up or marked price for a target profit

**How you spot it:** "How much above cost should a shopkeeper mark his goods so that after a 10% discount he still gains 8%?"

**Think:** work backwards. Start from the SP he needs (CP plus his profit). The SP is the marked price after the discount. Undo the discount to get the marked price.

**Example 1.** Cost is Rs 750. The shopkeeper wants a 20% profit after giving a 10% discount. What price should he mark?
- Needed SP $=750\times1.2=900$.
- SP is 90% of MP, so $\text{MP}=\dfrac{900}{0.9}=$ **Rs 1000**.

**Example 2.** What percentage above cost must goods be marked to gain 8% after a 10% discount?
- Take CP $=100$. SP needed $=108$.
- $\text{MP}=\dfrac{108}{0.9}=120$. So **20% above cost**.

**Example 3.** Goods are marked 20% above cost. What discount still gives 8% profit?
- CP $=100$, MP $=120$, needed SP $=108$.
- SP is $\dfrac{108}{120}=0.9$ of MP, so the discount is **10%**.

**Your turn:** how much above cost must a shopkeeper mark to gain 12% after a 20% discount? (Answer: 40%. $\dfrac{112}{0.8}=140$.)

### Type 9. Profit if there were no discount

**How you spot it:** "A trader gains 8% after giving a 10% discount. What would his gain be if he sold at the marked price?"

**Think:** the same article. The SP with discount is $1.08\times\text{CP}$ and also $0.9\times\text{MP}$. Link them, and find MP in terms of CP.

**Example 1.** Gain 8% after 10% discount.
- CP $=100$. SP $=108$ and SP $=0.9\times\text{MP}$.
- $\text{MP}=\dfrac{108}{0.9}=120$.
- Selling at MP would gain **20%**.

**Example 2.** Gain 20% after a 25% discount.
- CP $=100$. SP $=120$. $\text{MP}=\dfrac{120}{0.75}=160$.
- Gain at marked price $=$ **60%**.

**Your turn:** a trader gains 17% after a 10% discount. His gain at the marked price? (Answer: 30%. $\dfrac{117}{0.9}=130$.)

### Type 10. Cost given as a share of the marked price, or finding cost from MP

**How you spot it:** "The cost price is 80% of the marked price. A discount of 10% is given. Find the profit percentage."

**Think:** put both CP and SP in terms of MP. Compare SP with CP.

**Example 1.** CP $=80\%$ of MP, discount 10%.
- Take MP $=100$. CP $=80$. SP $=90$.
- Profit $=10$ on cost 80. $\dfrac{10}{80}\times100=$ **12.5%**.

**Example 2.** MP is Rs 1200. A discount of 10% is given and the trader gains 8%. Find the cost price.
- SP $=1200\times0.9=1080$.
- $\text{CP}=\dfrac{1080}{1.08}=$ **Rs 1000**.

**Your turn:** CP is 60% of MP and a discount of 25% is given. Profit percentage? (Answer: 25%. MP 100, CP 60, SP 75, and $\dfrac{15}{60}=25\%$.)

### Type 11. "Buy x get y free"

**How you spot it:** "Buy 4 get 1 free", "5 pens for the price of 4", "a dozen at the price of 10".

**Think:** a free article is a discount. You pay for some, but take more. The discount percentage is **free items divided by items you take home**.

**Example 1.** Buy 4 get 1 free. You take home 5 and pay for 4.
- Discount $=\dfrac{1}{5}=$ **20%**.

**Example 2.** Pens cost Rs 20 each. Buy 5 and get 1 free.
- 6 pens at Rs 20 would be Rs 120. You pay Rs 100.
- Discount $=\dfrac{20}{120}=$ **$16\frac{2}{3}\%$**.

**Example 3.** Which is better: 20% off, or buy 3 get 1 free?
- Buy 3 get 1 free is $\dfrac{1}{4}=25\%$.
- 25% beats 20%, so **buy 3 get 1 free is better**.

**Exam twist:** the base is the number you **take**, not the number you pay. Buy 4 get 1 free is not 25%.

**Your turn:** buy 5, get 3 free. Discount percentage? (Answer: 37.5%. You take 8 and pay for 5, so $\dfrac{3}{8}$.)

### Type 12. Overall discount on several items

**How you spot it:** "A shop sells a shirt at 10% off and trousers at 20% off. What is the overall discount?"

**Think:** the overall discount is total rupees saved over total marked price. Do not average the two percentages unless the marked values are equal.

**Example 1.** Shirt MP Rs 800 at 10% off. Trousers MP Rs 1200 at 20% off.
- Paid: $800\times0.9=720$ and $1200\times0.8=960$. Total Rs 1680.
- Total MP $=2000$. Discount $=320$.
- $\dfrac{320}{2000}\times100=$ **16%**.

**Example 2.** A quarter of a stock (by marked value) is sold at 20% off and the rest at 8% off. Overall discount?
- $\dfrac{1}{4}\times20+\dfrac{3}{4}\times8=5+6=$ **11%**.

**Your turn:** goods of marked value Rs 600 are sold at 30% off and goods of marked value Rs 400 at 20% off. Overall discount? (Answer: 26%. Saved $180+80=260$, out of 1000.)

### Type 13. Discount given as a ratio or as a share of the selling price

**How you spot it:** "The marked price and the selling price are in the ratio 5 : 4." Or "the discount is 25% of the selling price".

**Think:** put MP and SP in units. The discount is the gap between them. Then divide by the **MP** units.

**Example 1.** MP : SP $=5:4$.
- Discount $=5-4=1$ unit out of 5. That is $\dfrac{1}{5}=$ **20%**.

**Example 2.** The discount is 25% of the selling price. Find the discount percentage.
- Let SP $=100$. Discount $=25$. MP $=125$.
- $\dfrac{25}{125}\times100=$ **20%**.

**Exam twist:** the second example is a favourite trap. The discount is 25% of SP, but it is only 20% of MP.

**Your turn:** MP : SP $=8:7$. Discount percentage? (Answer: 12.5%. One unit out of 8.)

### Type 14. Tax (GST) added after a discount

**How you spot it:** "After a discount of 15%, GST of 10% is added. Find the final bill."

**Think:** GST is charged on the price **after** the discount. Multiply the multipliers: discount first, then $1+\text{tax rate}$.

**Example 1.** MP Rs 8000, discount 15%, then 10% GST.
- $8000\times0.85\times1.1=$ **Rs 7480**.

**Example 2.** The final bill is Rs 5940 after a 25% discount and then 10% GST. Find the marked price.
- Undo the tax: $\dfrac{5940}{1.1}=5400$.
- Undo the discount: $\dfrac{5400}{0.75}=$ **Rs 7200**.

**Your turn:** MP Rs 4000, discount 20%, then 5% GST on the discounted price. Final bill? (Answer: Rs 3360. $4000\times0.8=3200$, and $3200\times1.05=3360$.)

### Type 15. Finding a missing discount

**How you spot it:** "The net discount is 28%. If the first discount is 20%, find the second." Or "Two equal successive discounts make 19%. Find each."

**Think:** work in "you pay" fractions. Divide the final fraction by the known one.

**Example 1.** Net discount 28%, first discount 20%.
- You finally pay 72%. After the first you pay 80%.
- Second multiplier $=\dfrac{0.72}{0.8}=0.9$. Second discount is **10%**.

**Example 2.** Discounts of 10% and 20% are given and a third discount makes the net discount 46%. Find it.
- Final pay $=0.54$. After two: $0.9\times0.8=0.72$.
- Third multiplier $=\dfrac{0.54}{0.72}=0.75$. Third discount is **25%**.

**Example 3.** Two equal successive discounts equal a single discount of 19%. Find each.
- Pay 81%, so each multiplier is $\sqrt{0.81}=0.9$.
- Each discount is **10%**.

**Your turn:** net discount is 40% and the first discount is 25%. Second? (Answer: 20%. $\dfrac{0.6}{0.75}=0.8$.)

## 4. One page to revise before the exam

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| MP and discount % given | SP $=\text{MP}\times\frac{100-d}{100}$ |
| MP and SP given | Discount % $=\frac{\text{MP}-\text{SP}}{\text{MP}}\times100$ |
| SP and discount % given | MP $=\frac{\text{SP}}{1-d/100}$ |
| Two successive discounts | Net $=a+b-\frac{ab}{100}$ |
| Three successive discounts | Apply the net formula twice |
| Comparing offers | Turn each into a single net discount |
| Percent and flat coupon | Order matters. Read which comes first |
| Marked above CP, then discount | CP $=100$, multiply, never subtract percents |
| Mark-up for target profit | Needed SP, then divide by $(1-d)$ |
| Profit without discount | Link SP $=$ CP $\times$ profit factor and SP $=$ MP $\times$ discount factor |
| Buy x get y free | Discount $=\frac{y}{x+y}$ |
| Several items | Total saved over total MP |
| MP : SP $=a:b$ | Discount $=\frac{a-b}{a}$ |
| GST after discount | Multiply by $(1+\text{tax})$ last |
| Missing discount | Divide final fraction by known fraction |

Memory table to learn:

| Discount | SP as fraction of MP | MP : SP |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | $\frac{9}{10}$ | $10:9$ |
| 12.5% | $\frac{7}{8}$ | $8:7$ |
| 20% | $\frac{4}{5}$ | $5:4$ |
| 25% | $\frac{3}{4}$ | $4:3$ |
| $33\frac{1}{3}\%$ | $\frac{2}{3}$ | $3:2$ |
| 50% | $\frac{1}{2}$ | $2:1$ |

## 5. Mistakes that cost marks

1. **Adding successive discounts.** 20% followed by 10% is 28%, not 30%.
2. **Dividing by SP when finding discount percent.** The base is always the **marked price**.
3. **Mixing the two bases.** Discount is on MP. Profit and loss are on CP. Never subtract a discount percent from a mark-up percent.
4. **Wrong direction for MP from SP.** SP is smaller than MP, so MP is SP divided by the fraction, not multiplied.
5. **Ignoring the order of a flat coupon.** Rs 200 off before and after a percent discount give different totals.
6. **Calling "buy 4 get 1 free" 25%.** You take 5 items, so the discount is $\dfrac{1}{5}=20\%$.
7. **Adding GST on the marked price.** Tax is charged on the discounted price, unless the question says otherwise.
