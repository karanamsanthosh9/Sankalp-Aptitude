# Time and Work

> Read this like a class, not like a formula list. Every idea comes with a picture in your head, then a solved example, then one for you to try. Do the "Your turn" boxes with a pen before reading the answer next to them.

## 0. Start here: what is this topic really about?

Imagine a wall that needs to be built. One mason builds it in 12 days, another in 18 days. The questions in this chapter ask things like:

- How long if both work together?
- What if one joins after 2 days?
- What if one leaves in the middle?
- What if the work is shared between men, women and pipes that fill tanks?

Every question, however long it looks, is the same story: **some work, some workers, some time**. If you can answer "how much does each worker do in ONE day?", you can answer everything in this chapter.

**What the exam gives you:** how long each person takes alone, or how long a group takes together, or how many workers and days. **What it asks for:** a time, a number of workers, a share of money, or a fraction of work.

**How many marks to expect:** SSC, RRB, banking and PSU papers usually carry 1 to 3 questions from this chapter. They are among the most scorable questions in the paper, because the method does not change.

## 1. The only idea you must understand

### One day's work

If a mason finishes a wall in 12 days, how much of the wall does he finish in one day?

One twelfth of it. Written as a fraction, $\dfrac{1}{12}$ of the wall per day. We call this his **rate**.

- Takes 5 days, then the rate is $\dfrac{1}{5}$ per day.
- Takes $x$ days, then the rate is $\dfrac{1}{x}$ per day.

**Remember:** a bigger time means a smaller rate. The slower the worker, the less he does each day.

### Two people together

When two people work together, each still does his own share every day. So their daily work **adds up**:

$$\text{Combined rate} = \text{Rate of A} + \text{Rate of B}$$

Once you know the combined rate, the time for the whole job is:

$$\text{Time} = \dfrac{1}{\text{Combined rate}}$$

### Trying it: A takes 12 days, B takes 18 days. Together?

Step 1. A does $\dfrac{1}{12}$ per day. B does $\dfrac{1}{18}$ per day.

Step 2. Together: $\dfrac{1}{12}+\dfrac{1}{18}=\dfrac{3}{36}+\dfrac{2}{36}=\dfrac{5}{36}$ per day.

Step 3. Time $=\dfrac{36}{5}=7.2$ days.

**Answer: 7.2 days.** That makes sense: together they must be faster than the faster one alone (12 days), so 7.2 is reasonable.

**Sense check you should always do:** the answer for "together" must be smaller than the smaller time given. If your answer is bigger, you have made a mistake.

### Your turn

A takes 6 days, B takes 3 days. Together? (Answer: 2 days)

## 2. The shortcut that saves 30 seconds in every question: the LCM trick

Fractions waste time. Here is how toppers avoid them.

### The idea in one picture

Say the job is making **36 bricks**. Why 36? Because 12 and 18 both divide into 36.

- A makes the 36 bricks in 12 days, so A makes **3 bricks a day**.
- B makes the 36 bricks in 18 days, so B makes **2 bricks a day**.
- Together: 5 bricks a day.
- 36 bricks at 5 a day: $\dfrac{36}{5}=7.2$ days.

Same answer, no fractions. The total work does not have to be real. You may call it **36 units**.

### The method (use this everywhere)

1. Take the **LCM of the times given** as the total work.
2. Each person's rate $=\dfrac{\text{total work}}{\text{time}}$. These are whole numbers.
3. Add rates for people working together.
4. Time $=\dfrac{\text{work}}{\text{rate}}$.

### Example: A, B, C take 10, 12 and 15 days. Together?

- LCM of 10, 12, 15 is **60** units.
- A: $60\div10=6$ per day. B: $60\div12=5$ per day. C: $60\div15=4$ per day.
- Together: $6+5+4=15$ per day.
- Time: $60\div15=$ **4 days**.

### Your turn

A takes 20 days, B takes 30. Together? (LCM is 60, rates 3 and 2. Answer: 12 days)

**Tip for the exam:** if the total work is not given, always pick the LCM. If a question mentions a number that already looks like a good total (say 120 units of wall), use that instead.

## 3. Types of questions and how to crack each one

The next sections go through every type you can meet. Each has: how you spot it, what to do, an example, and an exam twist.

### Type 1. Finding the partner's time

**How you spot it:** "A and B together can finish in 6 days. A alone takes 10. How long does B take alone?"

**Think:** together = A + B, so B = together minus A.

**Solve it:**
- LCM of 6 and 10 is 30 units.
- Together: $30\div6=5$ per day. A: $30\div10=3$ per day.
- B: $5-3=2$ per day.
- B alone: $30\div2=$ **15 days**.

**Shortcut for two people:** B alone $=\dfrac{x\cdot t}{x-t}$, where $x$ is A's time and $t$ is the together time. Check: $\dfrac{10\times6}{10-6}=15$.

**Your turn:** A and B together take 8 days. A alone takes 12. B alone? (Answer: 24 days)

### Type 2. Efficiency questions ("50% more efficient", "twice as fast")

**How you spot it:** words like efficient, faster, more skilled, "A is 3 times as good as B".

**The one thing to remember:** efficiency and time work in **opposite** directions. A better worker needs less time.

If A : B efficiency is $3:2$, then A : B time is $2:3$.

**Example 1.** A is 50% more efficient than B. B takes 24 days. How long does A take?

- "50% more" means A does 150 when B does 100. Ratio $150:100=3:2$.
- Efficiency A : B $=3:2$, so time A : B $=2:3$.
- A's time $=24\times\dfrac{2}{3}=$ **16 days**.

**Example 2.** A is twice as fast as B. Together they take 12 days. How long does each take alone?

- Rates are $2$ and $1$. Together $=3$ units a day.
- Total work $=3\times12=36$ units.
- A: $36\div2=$ **18 days**. B: $36\div1=$ **36 days**.

**Exam twist:** the question may say "A takes 20% less time than B". Then A's time is $80\%$ of B's. Do not confuse "20% more efficient" (rate up 20%) with "20% less time" (time down 20%).

**Your turn:** A is 25% more efficient than B. B takes 20 days. A alone? (Answer: 16 days)

### Type 3. Three people, with pairs given

**How you spot it:** "A and B take 12 days, B and C take 15 days, C and A take 20 days."

**Think:** if you add all three pair rates, you count each person **twice**. So halve it to get A + B + C. Then subtract a pair to get the third person.

**Solve it:**
- LCM of 12, 15, 20 is 60 units.
- A+B $=5$, B+C $=4$, C+A $=3$.
- A+B+C $=\dfrac{5+4+3}{2}=6$ per day. All three together: $60\div6=$ **10 days**.
- A $=6-4=2$ per day, so A alone **30 days**.
- B $=6-3=3$ per day, so B alone **20 days**.
- C $=6-5=1$ per day, so C alone **60 days**.

**Your turn:** A+B take 10 days, B+C take 15, A+C take 12. All three together? (Answer: 8 days)

### Type 4. Someone joins late or leaves early

This is the most common type. The trick: **work in stages**, and always find how much work is done before changing the team.

**Example 1: joins late.** A takes 10 days, B takes 15. A works alone for 2 days, then B joins. Total time?

- LCM 30 units. A $=3$ per day, B $=2$ per day.
- Stage 1: A alone for 2 days does $3\times2=6$ units. Left: $30-6=24$.
- Stage 2: together $5$ per day. Time $=\dfrac{24}{5}=4.8$ days.
- Total $=2+4.8=$ **6.8 days**.

**Example 2: leaves early.** A takes 20 days, B takes 30. They start together, and A leaves after 6 days. Total time?

- LCM 60 units. A $=3$, B $=2$, together $5$.
- Stage 1: 6 days together $=30$ units. Left: $30$.
- Stage 2: B alone at 2 per day. $30\div2=15$ days.
- Total $=6+15=$ **21 days**.

**Example 3: leaves a few days before the end.** A takes 20 days, B takes 30. They work together, but A leaves 5 days before the job is finished. Total time?

- Let the total time be $T$ days. B works all $T$ days. A works only $T-5$ days.
- Work done by B $+$ work done by A $=60$ units: $2T+3(T-5)=60$.
- $5T-15=60$, so $5T=75$ and $T=$ **15 days**.

**Your turn:** A takes 10 days, B takes 15. They work together for 3 days, then A leaves. Total time? (Answer: 10.5 days. LCM 30: A $=3$, B $=2$. Three days of both $=15$, left $15$, B alone $15\div2=7.5$ days, so $3+7.5$.)

### Type 5. Alternate days

**How you spot it:** "A and B work on alternate days, A starting."

**Think:** treat two days as **one cycle**. Find the work in one cycle, count how many full cycles fit, then handle the leftover day by day.

**Example.** A takes 6 days, B takes 12. They work on alternate days, A starting first. When is the job done?

- LCM 12 units. A $=2$, B $=1$ per day.
- One cycle (A then B) does $2+1=3$ units.
- After 3 cycles (6 days): $9$ units. Left: $3$.
- Day 7 is A's turn: $2$ units, total $11$. Day 8 is B's turn: $1$ unit, total $12$. Done.
- **Answer: 8 days.**

**Warning:** do not answer 7.5. A worker does not "half-day" a turn unless the question says so. When the job ends in the middle of a turn, the full turn day is counted, unless the question asks for the exact time.

**Your turn:** A takes 6 days, B takes 4. They alternate, A first. Total days? (Answer: 5 days. LCM 12: A $=2$, B $=3$, cycle $=5$, after 4 days $=10$, day 5 is A's turn $=12$.)

### Type 6. Fraction of work in some days

**How you spot it:** "A does $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of the job in 5 days."

**Think:** if someone does a fraction $f$ of the job in $d$ days, the full job takes him $d\div f$ days.

**Example.** A does $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of a job in 5 days. B does $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of it in 6 days. How long together?

- A alone: $5\div\dfrac{1}{3}=15$ days.
- B alone: $6\div\dfrac{2}{5}=15$ days.
- Together: $\dfrac{15}{2}=$ **7.5 days**.

**Your turn:** A does $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of a job in 3 days. B does $\dfrac{1}{3}$ in 4 days. Together? (Answer: 6 days. Both take 12 days alone.)

### Type 7. Men and days

**How you spot it:** "12 men finish a job in 15 days. How long will 20 men take?"

**Think:** the total job in **man-days** never changes. More men means fewer days.

$$\text{Men}\times\text{Days}=\text{constant}$$

**Example 1.** 12 men finish in 15 days. How long for 20 men?
- Job $=12\times15=180$ man-days.
- $180\div20=$ **9 days**.

**Example 2: workers leave midway.** 20 workers finish a job in 18 days. After 6 days, 5 leave. How many days in total?
- Job $=20\times18=360$ man-days.
- Done in 6 days: $20\times6=120$. Left: $240$.
- Now 15 workers: $240\div15=16$ days.
- Total $=6+16=$ **22 days**.

**Example 3: absentees.** 20 men planned to finish a job in 30 days, but it took 40 days because some were absent. How many were absent?
- Job $=20\times30=600$ man-days.
- Men who actually worked: $600\div40=15$.
- Absent $=20-15=$ **5 men**.

**Your turn:** 10 men finish a job in 12 days. After 4 days, 5 more men join. Total days? (Answer: $9\dfrac{1}{3}$ days. Job 120, done 40, left 80, 15 men, $5\dfrac{1}{3}$ days more.)

### Type 8. Men, days, hours, and different amounts of work

**How you spot it:** hours per day are mentioned, or the amount of work (metres of wall, number of items) changes.

**Think:** add the extra factors and compare.

$$\dfrac{M_1\times D_1\times H_1}{W_1}=\dfrac{M_2\times D_2\times H_2}{W_2}$$

($M$ men, $D$ days, $H$ hours a day, $W$ amount of work.)

**Example 1: hours.** 12 men working 8 hours a day finish a job in 15 days. How many days for 10 men working 9 hours a day?
- $12\times8\times15=10\times9\times d$.
- $1440=90d$, so $d=$ **16 days**.

**Example 2: different work.** 8 men build 120 m of wall in 15 days. How many men are needed to build 200 m in 20 days?
- $\dfrac{8\times15}{120}=\dfrac{M\times20}{200}$.
- $1=\dfrac{M}{10}$, so $M=$ **10 men**.

**Your turn:** 15 men working 6 hours a day finish a job in 16 days. How many days for 12 men working 8 hours a day? (Answer: 15 days)

### Type 9. Men, women and children

**How you spot it:** "2 men do the work of 3 women."

**Think:** convert everyone into one unit first.

**Example.** 2 men do the work of 3 women. 4 men and 6 women finish a job in 10 days. How long will 5 women take alone?
- 2 men $=3$ women, so 1 man $=1.5$ women. 4 men $=6$ women.
- The team is $6+6=12$ women. Job $=12\times10=120$ woman-days.
- 5 women: $120\div5=$ **24 days**.

**Your turn:** 3 men do the work of 4 women. 6 men and 8 women finish a job in 6 days. How long for 4 women alone? (Answer: 24 days. 6 men $=8$ women, team $=16$ women, job $=96$, $96\div4$.)

### Type 10. Wages

**How you spot it:** "They together earn Rs 1500. How much does each get?"

**Think:** money is split in the ratio of **work done**, not just days attended. If they work the same number of days, work done is in the ratio of their rates.

**Example 1.** A takes 12 days, B takes 18 days. Together they earn Rs 1500. Share of each?
- Rates: $\dfrac{1}{12}:\dfrac{1}{18}=3:2$.
- A: $\dfrac{3}{5}\times1500=$ **Rs 900**. B: $\dfrac{2}{5}\times1500=$ **Rs 600**.

**Example 2.** A, B and C take 6, 8 and 12 days and are paid Rs 2700 together. C's share?
- LCM 24: rates $4,3,2$. Ratio $4:3:2$.
- C: $\dfrac{2}{9}\times2700=$ **Rs 600**.

**Exam twist:** if one person works for fewer days, multiply his rate by his days first. A person who joined late earns less because he did less work.

**Your turn:** same A, B, C as Example 2. A's share? (Answer: Rs 1200)

### Type 11. Pipes, tanks and leaks

**How you spot it:** pipes filling or emptying a tank.

**Think:** exactly the same as workers. A filling pipe is a worker who does positive work. An emptying pipe, or a leak, is a worker who **undoes** the work, so its rate is negative.

**Example 1.** An inlet fills a tank in 6 hours. An outlet empties it in 10 hours. Both are open. How long to fill?
- $\dfrac{1}{6}-\dfrac{1}{10}=\dfrac{1}{15}$. So **15 hours**.

**Example 2.** A pump fills a tank in 8 hours. With a leak it takes 10 hours. How long does the leak alone take to empty a full tank?
- Leak's rate $=\dfrac{1}{8}-\dfrac{1}{10}=\dfrac{1}{40}$. So **40 hours**.

**Example 3.** Pipes A and B fill a tank in 12 and 15 hours. Pipe C empties it in 20 hours. All three are open.
- LCM 60: A $=5$, B $=4$, C $=-3$. Net $=6$ per hour.
- $60\div6=$ **10 hours**.

**Your turn:** a pump fills a tank in 6 hours. A leak makes it take 8 hours. How long does the leak alone take to empty it? (Answer: 24 hours)

### Type 12. The "time difference" type

**How you spot it:** "A alone takes 10 days less than B. Together they take 12 days."

**Method 1 (equation).** Let A take $x$ days, so B takes $x+10$.
- $\dfrac{1}{x}+\dfrac{1}{x+10}=\dfrac{1}{12}$.
- $12(2x+10)=x(x+10)$, so $x^2-14x-120=0$.
- $(x-20)(x+6)=0$, so $x=$ **20 days** (B $=30$).

**Method 2 (shortcut, when it fits).** If A takes $a$ days more than the together time, and B takes $b$ days more, then $t=\sqrt{ab}$.

**Check on a known case.** A takes 28 days, B takes 21 days, and together they take 12 days. A is $28-12=16$ more, and B is $21-12=9$ more. $\sqrt{16\times9}=12$. It works.

**Your turn:** A takes 6 days less than B. Together they take 4 days. A alone? (Answer: 6 days. $x^2-2x-24=0$, so $x=6$.)

## 4. One page to revise before the exam

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Different times given | Take the LCM as total work, so rates are whole numbers. |
| Together | Add the rates. |
| Find the partner | Together rate minus the known rate. |
| Pairs given | Add the three pairs, halve, then subtract each pair. |
| More efficient | Time ratio is the inverse of efficiency ratio. |
| Someone joins or leaves | Work in stages. Subtract the work already done. |
| Leaves before the end | Let total time be $T$. Each person works their own days. |
| Alternate days | One cycle first, then the leftover turn by turn. |
| Men, days | $\text{Men}\times\text{Days}$ stays constant. |
| Hours or different work | $\dfrac{MDH}{W}$ on both sides. |
| Wages | Split in the ratio of work done. |
| Leak or outlet | Negative rate. |

## 5. Mistakes that cost marks

1. **Adding times instead of rates.** A takes 12, B takes 18: the answer is not 30. Add rates.
2. **Using the wrong direction for efficiency.** More efficient means less time.
3. **Forgetting what is already done.** When the team changes, subtract the finished work first.
4. **Half-day answers in alternating problems.** Count full turns.
5. **Mixing hours and days.** Keep all times in the same unit.
6. **Sharing wages by days only.** Share by work done.
